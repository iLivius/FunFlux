    BLAST hit depth¶

    This page explains how many BLAST hits FunFlux keeps for each contig, which e-value the search uses, and why. Read it before you change parameters.blast_max_target_seqs or parameters.blast_max_hsps, when you update from FunFlux 2.0.0, or when you compare genus calls with those of FunFlux 2.0.0 or 1.1.x.

    Terms used on this page
    the screen the contamination and mitochondrion screen of the draft assembly: BLAST, BlobTools, the mitochondrion screen and the contig selector; in contigs mode only with parameters.contigs.screen: true
    draft assembly the assembly the screen examines, before the screen removes any contig: assembly.fasta in longread and hybrid mode, and contigs_filt.fasta, the contigs after FunFlux's contig filter, in illumina mode, for the hybrid guide and in contigs mode; not yet polished
    guide assembly in hybrid mode, the SPAdes assembly of the Illumina reads; it gets a screen of its own and is not delivered
    contig selector rule select_contigs, the last step of the screen: it keeps or removes each contig by genus, as parameters.decontamination says; the default, off, keeps every contig
    core_nt the NCBI core nucleotide BLAST database, which the screen searches every draft contig against; you download it, with its taxonomy files, into directories.blast_db
    subject the BLAST word for a database sequence a contig matched, the contig being the query; BLAST keeps up to 10 per contig (blast_max_target_seqs; BLAST's own option, -max_target_seqs, calls them target sequences)
    HSP high-scoring segment pair, one local alignment between a contig and a subject; BLAST keeps up to blast_max_hsps (5) per subject
    hit one line of the BLAST table: one HSP between a contig and a subject
    bit score BLAST's score for one HSP: the longer and closer the match, the higher the score
    e-value BLAST's estimate of how often a match at least this good would turn up by chance in a search of the whole database; the search drops matches above its cut-off, so 1e-10 is stricter than 1e-5
    bestsum the rule BlobTools uses to give each contig a taxon: it adds up the bit scores of all the contig's hits per taxon and takes the taxon with the highest sum
    direct hit a hit to a subject whose title names a mitochondrial genome, such as "mitochondrion, complete genome"; the mitochondrion screen counts only these (Mitochondria lists the wordings)
    FunFluxL, FunFluxL+ two of the v1 workflows that FunFlux v2 replaces: FunFluxL for long reads and the unpublished FunFluxL+ for hybrid data
    clean circle a contig the assembler recorded as circular, not as a repeat and not as an alternative copy of another contig
    genus filter the setting parameters.decontamination.mode: include or exclude, under which the contig selector keeps or removes contigs by the genus BlobTools gave them
    MGISEQ a short-read sequencer from MGI Tech that produces paired reads like Illumina's

    The contamination and mitochondrion screen runs one BLAST search per draft assembly. Rule blast_contigs runs it with megablast against core_nt and an e-value of 1e-10. In hybrid mode, rule blast_guide_contigs does the same for the guide assembly, with the same blast_max_target_seqs and blast_max_hsps. Two programs read the result table, BlobTools and the mitochondrion screen:

    ---

  themeVariables:
    fontSize: 14px

    nodeSpacing: 35

flowchart TD
    C([draft contigs]) --> B[megablast against core_nt<br/>blast_contigs]
    B --> T([BLAST table<br/>by default at most 10 subjects<br/>per contig, 5 HSPs per subject])
    T --> BT[BlobTools<br/>assigns each contig a genus]
    T --> MS[mitochondrion screen<br/>looks for mitochondrial contigs]

    Two BLAST options decide how much of the search is kept. By default FunFlux sets them to 10 and 5 in every mode.

    Key Default blastn option blastn help text (BLAST+ 2.16.0)
    parameters.blast_max_target_seqs 10 -max_target_seqs "Maximum number of aligned sequences to keep (value of 5 or more is recommended)"
    parameters.blast_max_hsps 5 -max_hsps "Set maximum number of HSPs per subject sequence to save for each query"
    Workflow or source max_target_seqs / max_hsps e-value
    FunFlux 1.1.x (Illumina) 10 / 1 1e-5
    FunFluxL and FunFluxL+ 50 / 5 1e-5
    FunFlux 2.0.0, every mode 50 / 5 1e-5
    FunFlux 2.1.0, every mode 10 / 5 1e-10
    BlobTools manual, for its "most accurate results" 10 / 1 1e-25
    BlobToolKit tutorial "Adding hits" 10 / 1 1e-25
    Sanger Tree of Life BlobToolKit pipeline 10 / 1 1e-10

    FunFlux keeps 10 subjects per contig, as the three BlobTools sources do, and searches with the e-value of the Sanger Tree of Life pipeline, 1e-10. Where the sources keep one HSP per subject, FunFlux keeps 5, because its mitochondrion screen reads the same table and needs more than one HSP per subject (below). The sources do not explain their values.

    FunFlux 2.0.0 kept 50 subjects with an e-value of 1e-5, the values of FunFluxL. With 50 subjects, a genus with many database entries of one gene, such as the same gene sequenced from many isolates, can outvote the genus of a contig's closest hits (Ten subjects). The e-value made almost no difference in the comparison: a close match over hundreds of bases has an e-value far below 1e-5, 1e-10 and 1e-25 alike, so the cut-off drops only weak or short matches.

    Five HSPs: what the mitochondrion screen needs¶

    The mitochondrion screen, rule screen_mitochondria, reads the BLAST column qcovs ("Query Coverage Per Subject"). qcovs is the share of the contig covered by all the HSPs that BLAST saved for one subject. With -max_hsps 1 only the best HSP is saved, and qcovs equals qcovhsp ("Query Coverage Per HSP"). Rearrangements, indels or a different start position on the circle can make a mitochondrial genome align to its closest reference in several blocks. With one HSP per subject, such a genome then looks partly covered.

    For each contig, the mitochondrion screen takes the highest qcovs among its direct hits and confirms the contig as follows:

    Mode Evidence from the assembler A contig is confirmed when its highest qcovs
    contigs, illumina, the hybrid guide none reaches mitochondria.min_direct_blast_qcov (default 35). A contig with a weaker direct hit becomes a mitochondrial_candidate; in contigs and illumina mode it stays in contigs_final.fasta and goes through the nuclear gene prediction
    longread, the hybrid long-read assembly the depth, or a clean circle reaches mitochondria.very_strong_qcov (70) on its own, or min_direct_blast_qcov (35) when the contig also has a high depth (at least min_assembler_coverage_ratio, 3.0, times the median) or is a clean circle

    One HSP per subject can leave a complete mitochondrial genome unconfirmed

    With one HSP per subject, only the best block of a multi-block match counts. A complete mitochondrial genome that aligns to its nearest core_nt reference in many pieces can then fall below min_direct_blast_qcov. The mitochondrion screen is described on Mitochondria.

    The default of 5 HSPs has not been compared with other values on a public genome.

    Ten subjects: what the genus calls need¶

    BLAST applies -max_target_seqs during the search, not as a filter on the finished, ranked list of hits. A small value can therefore drop subjects that would have ranked higher (Shah et al. 2019). NCBI's reply confirmed the mechanism, fixed a related bug in BLAST+ 2.8.1 and made BLAST+ warn when fewer than five subjects are requested (Madden et al. 2019). BLAST+ 2.16.0 prints:

    Warning: [blastn] Examining 5 or more matches is recommended

    FunFlux's 10 meets that advice. More subjects are not better for the genus calls, because bestsum adds up the bit scores of every hit per genus. A genus with many database entries of one gene, such as the same gene sequenced from many isolates of one species, then gains the scores of every entry, while the genus with the closest hits may have only a few entries. With 10 subjects, at most 10 entries of one gene can enter the sum. That weakens the effect; it does not remove it.

    One contig of Cordyceps cateniannulata GXU-8616

    The HiFi assembly of the validation runs has a contig of 4.9 Mb. With 50 subjects, 41 of them were Beauveria (38 of them B. bassiana), all the same gene (carbamoyl-phosphate synthase) sequenced from different isolates, with bit scores of 7,062 to 7,306 each. Six were Cordyceps, including the best single hit (bit score 10,567). The sums were 296,140 for Beauveria and 53,026 for Cordyceps, and BlobTools called the contig Beauveria. With 10 subjects (five Cordyceps, three Beauveria, two Akanthomyces), the sums were 45,921 for Cordyceps and 21,879 for Beauveria, and the call was Cordyceps, the strain's genus.

    FunFlux 2.0.0 and 2.1.0 compared¶

    The table compares the setting of FunFlux 2.0.0 (50 subjects, e-value 1e-5) with that of FunFlux 2.1.0 (10 subjects, e-value 1e-10). Both keep 5 HSPs per subject. The four draft assemblies were made from the public reads of the validation runs, and the screen ran on them in contigs mode, so BLAST was the only evidence of the mitochondrion screen. B. cinerea is well represented in core_nt. C. cateniannulata itself has few sequences there, so its contigs are called from its relatives: other Cordyceps species, but also other genera such as Beauveria. "Genus changed" is the share of the assembly's bases in contigs whose genus differs between the two settings.

    B. cinerea B05.10, ONT reads, hifiasm 34 0.17 % Botrytis 97.6 % 97.7 %
    C. cateniannulata GXU-8616, MGISEQ reads, SPAdes 294 16.9 % Cordyceps 56.4 % 72.8 %
    C. cateniannulata GXU-8616, HiFi reads, hifiasm 19 27.9 % Cordyceps 46.4 % 74.3 %
    • On B. cinerea the two settings agree: a few small contigs change genus.
    • On C. cateniannulata 10 subjects change the genus of 17 % (MGISEQ) and 28 % (HiFi) of the assembly, almost all of it from Beauveria, Fusarium or Colletotrichum to Cordyceps. Some calls are still wrong. Two HiFi contigs (4.8 and 3.1 Mb) and twelve MGISEQ contigs (four of 0.8 to 2.8 Mb and eight small ones) stay Beauveria, about a quarter of each assembly. Each of the six large ones has its best single hit in Cordyceps, one of them in C. cateniannulata GXU-8616 itself. But four to nine of its 10 subjects are the same gene sequenced from many Beauveria isolates.
    • The e-value alone, 1e-10 instead of 1e-5 with 50 subjects, changed the genus of at most 0.03 % of the bases. Five short contigs (0.6 to 4.2 kb) lost their only hits and became no-hit. With the default, mode: off, they stay. With a genus filter they are now removed: include removes every no-hit contig, and exclude does so with discard_no_hit: true. FunFlux 2.0.0 had given four of them the strain's genus.
    • The mitochondrion screen confirmed the same mitochondrial contigs, with the same coverage, under both settings. Both strains' own mitochondrial genomes are in core_nt, and their mitochondrial contigs match them best. These runs were made before the species check was added: the three small contigs of human mitochondrial DNA in the B05.10 Illumina draft were confirmed under both settings too, and FunFlux 2.1.0 calls them foreign_mitochondrial (Validation). One contig of 32 kb was a mitochondrial_candidate with 50 subjects and not with 10: its direct hits, to mitochondrial genomes that each covered 1 % of it or less, were no longer among the 10 subjects. A candidate stays in the nuclear genome, so no genome file changed; only that row of mitochondrial_screen.tsv did.
    • Without the strain's own species in core_nt, as for a new species, the fungal mitochondrial contigs of the B05.10 Illumina draft and of both GXU-8616 drafts were searched again with that species left out. With it, their best direct hit covered 99 to 100 % of them. Without it, all three were still confirmed, with the same best coverage under both settings: 89 % for the B05.10 mitochondrion, and 50 % (HiFi draft, where the contig holds two copies of the mitochondrial genome end to end) and 72 % (MGISEQ draft) for GXU-8616. For GXU-8616 that best hit was a record titled "genome assembly, organelle: mitochondrion". The 10-subject search did miss some other mitochondrial genomes that scored higher than ones it kept, because BLAST applies the limit during the search, but not the best one. If no mitochondrion is confirmed, see Mitochondria.
    • Run time was the same with either setting: 44 to 63 minutes per draft on 24 threads, with both searches running at the same time on a shared server. The BLAST table is three to five times smaller with 10 subjects.

    The genus calls matter only with a genus filter, decontamination.mode include or exclude. With off, the default, every contig is kept whatever its genus, so the genus calls change no delivered genome file. See Decontamination.

    These are two genomes, so the numbers show the direction of the effect, not its size on another fungus.

    Updating from FunFlux 2.0.0¶

    • Your configuration file. The FunFlux 2.0.0 template, config/config.yaml, sets blast_max_target_seqs: 50, and updating FunFlux does not change the copy you made from it. Set it to 10, or delete the line, to search as FunFlux 2.1.0 does.
    • An output folder made with 2.0.0. Started from the same launch folder as before, FunFlux 2.1.0 runs the BLAST search there again, even if you keep blast_max_target_seqs: 50, because the BLAST command itself changed (its e-value) and Snakemake compares it with the one recorded in .snakemake/ of the launch folder. Every later step follows for each sample with the screen, including the whole annotation: hours per sample. With the default, mode: off, every contig is still kept, but the genes are predicted again, and two annotations of the same genome differ at about 1 % of the gene models (Run-to-run differences). Started from a new clone or another launch folder, Snakemake has no record of the old command and keeps the 2.0.0 BLAST table; add --forcerun blast_contigs (in hybrid also blast_guide_contigs) to search again. To keep finished 2.0.0 results as they are, leave that folder to FunFlux 2.0.0.
    • ONT samples in such a folder start again from the reads. FunFlux 2.1.0 trims the ONT adapter from the reads before Filtlong (Adapter trimming), so each ONT sample is trimmed, filtered and assembled again, and its genome can differ from the 2.0.0 one: on B05.10, trimming changed the mitochondrial contig (Validation). With ont_adapter: off in parameters.longread or parameters.hybrid, the reads are assembled untrimmed, as in 2.0.0.
    • A genus filter chosen from a 2.0.0 run may no longer fit, because some contigs change genus. Read the new contig_taxonomy_decisions.tsv before you filter again (Decontamination).

    Notices and re-runs¶

    Both values must be whole numbers of at least 1. With the screen on, two notices can print when the workflow starts:

    [FunFlux] NOTE: parameters.blast_max_hsps is 1. Below 5, BLAST reports the coverage of a multi-block mitochondrial match as that of its best block only, which weakens the mitochondrion screen (a complete mitogenome can fall under min_direct_blast_qcov).
[FunFlux] NOTE: parameters.blast_max_target_seqs is 3; BLAST recommends 5 or more.

    The first notice, on blast_max_hsps, prints when that value is below 5 and mitochondria.mode is not off. The second, on blast_max_target_seqs, prints when that value is below 5.

    Changing either value runs the BLAST search again (in hybrid both searches) and every step after it, including the whole annotation: hours per sample.

    Reproducing earlier versions¶

    Apart from the mitochondrion case described on Mitochondria, set these values only to compare with results of an earlier version. The e-value is fixed at 1e-10 and cannot be set, while the earlier versions searched with 1e-5; in the comparison above, the e-value changed the genus of at most 0.03 % of the bases.

    To come close to FunFlux 2.0.0, FunFluxL or FunFluxL+:

    parameters:
  blast_max_target_seqs: 50
  blast_max_hsps: 5

    To come close to FunFlux 1.1.x:

    parameters:
  blast_max_target_seqs: 10
  blast_max_hsps: 1

    FunFlux 1.1.x took 10 subjects and 1 HSP, but not the e-value, from the BlobToolKit tutorial "Adding hits", which prepares a BLAST table for the taxonomy only. These values print the blast_max_hsps notice, because the mitochondrion screen is weaker with one HSP. FunFlux v2 has not been tested with them.
