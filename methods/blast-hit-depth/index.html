    BLAST hit depth¶

    This page explains how many BLAST hits FunFlux keeps for each contig, and why. Read it before you change parameters.blast_max_target_seqs or parameters.blast_max_hsps, or when you compare genus calls with those of FunFlux 1.1.x.

    Terms used on this page
    the screen the contamination and mitochondrion screen of the draft: BLAST, BlobTools, the mitochondrion screen and the contig selector; in contigs mode only with parameters.contigs.screen: true
    core_nt the NCBI core nucleotide BLAST database, which the screen searches every draft contig against; you download it, with its taxonomy files, into directories.blast_db
    subject the BLAST word for a database sequence a contig matched, the contig being the query; BLAST keeps up to 50 per contig (blast_max_target_seqs)
    HSP high-scoring segment pair, one local alignment between a contig and a subject; BLAST keeps up to blast_max_hsps (5) per subject
    FunFluxL, FunFluxL+ two of the v1 workflows that FunFlux v2 replaces: FunFluxL for long reads and the unpublished FunFluxL+ for hybrid data
    clean circle a contig the assembler recorded as circular, not as a repeat and not as an alternative copy of another contig
    genus filter the setting parameters.decontamination.mode: include or exclude, under which the contig selector keeps or removes contigs by the genus BlobTools gave them

    The contamination and mitochondrion screen runs one BLAST search per draft assembly. Rule blast_contigs runs it with megablast against core_nt and an e-value of 1e-5, and in hybrid rule blast_guide_contigs does the same for the guide assembly, with the same two keys. Two programs read its result table, BlobTools and the mitochondrion screen:

    Two BLAST options decide how much of the search is kept. FunFlux v2 sets them to 50 and 5 in every mode.

    Key Default blastn option blastn help text (BLAST+ 2.16.0)
    parameters.blast_max_target_seqs 50 -max_target_seqs "Maximum number of aligned sequences to keep (value of 5 or more is recommended)"
    parameters.blast_max_hsps 5 -max_hsps "Set maximum number of HSPs per subject sequence to save for each query"
    Workflow or source max_target_seqs / max_hsps e-value
    FunFlux 1.1.x (Illumina) 10 / 1 1e-5
    FunFluxL and FunFluxL+ 50 / 5 1e-5
    FunFlux v2, every mode 50 / 5 1e-5
    BlobTools manual, for its "most accurate results" 10 / 1 1e-25
    BlobToolKit tutorial "Adding hits" 10 / 1 1e-25
    Sanger Tree of Life BlobToolKit pipeline 10 / 1 1e-10

    FunFlux v2 keeps the values of FunFluxL. They do not come from a published comparison, and the three BlobTools sources do not explain theirs either. The sources' 10 / 1 serves the taxonomy alone. FunFlux v2 also reads the table in its mitochondrion screen, which needs more than one HSP per subject (below). The e-value of 1e-5 is more permissive than the sources' 1e-10 or 1e-25. The difference matters only for weak, short matches, which can give a contig a genus where a stricter cut-off would leave it without a hit. A contig that matches a database sequence over hundreds of bases has an e-value far below all three cut-offs.

    FunFlux 1.1.x used 10 / 1, so its genus calls can differ from those of v2, as described in What changes with fewer hits.

    Five HSPs: what the mitochondrion screen needs¶

    The mitochondrion screen, rule screen_mitochondria, reads the BLAST column qcovs ("Query Coverage Per Subject"). qcovs is the share of the contig covered by all the HSPs that BLAST saved for one subject. With -max_hsps 1 only the best HSP is saved, and qcovs equals qcovhsp ("Query Coverage Per HSP"). Rearrangements, indels or a different start position on the circle can make a mitochondrial genome align to its closest reference in several blocks. With one HSP per subject, such a genome then looks partly covered.

    For each contig, the mitochondrion screen takes the highest qcovs among its hits to organelle genomes and confirms the contig as follows:

    Mode Evidence from the assembler A contig is confirmed when its highest qcovs
    contigs, illumina, the hybrid guide none reaches mitochondria.min_direct_blast_qcov (default 35). A contig with a weaker organelle hit becomes a mitochondrial_candidate, which stays in contigs_final.fasta and goes through the nuclear gene prediction
    longread, the hybrid long-read assembly the depth, or a clean circle reaches mitochondria.very_strong_qcov (70) on its own, or reaches min_direct_blast_qcov but then only together with the assembler's depth or a clean circle

    One HSP per subject can leave a complete mitochondrial genome unconfirmed

    With one HSP per subject, only the best block of a multi-block match counts. A complete mitochondrial genome that aligns to its nearest core_nt reference in many pieces can then fall below min_direct_blast_qcov. The mitochondrion screen is described on Mitochondria.

    The default of 5 HSPs has not been compared with other values on a public genome.

    Fifty target sequences: BLAST's own advice¶

    BLAST applies -max_target_seqs during the search, not as a filter on the finished, ranked list of hits. A small value can therefore drop subjects that would have ranked higher (Shah et al. 2019). NCBI's reply confirmed the mechanism, fixed a related bug in BLAST+ 2.8.1 and made BLAST+ warn when fewer than five subjects are requested (Madden et al. 2019). BLAST+ 2.16.0 prints:

    Warning: [blastn] Examining 5 or more matches is recommended

    FunFlux uses 50, the value of FunFluxL; the BlobTools sources in the table above use 10. Both meet NCBI's advice of at least five.

    What changes with fewer hits¶

    FunFlux 1.1.x took 10 / 1 from the BlobToolKit tutorial "Adding hits", which prepares a BLAST table for the taxonomy only, with -max_target_seqs 10 -max_hsps 1 -evalue 1e-25. FunFlux v2 also reads the table for its mitochondrion screen, which needs the coverage that one HSP per subject cannot measure. Keeping fewer subjects and HSPs has these effects:

    • Genus calls. BlobTools' bestsum rule sums the bit scores of all saved hits per taxon. The number of subjects and HSPs kept can therefore change the genus a contig is assigned to. This matters most for a fungus whose own genus has few reference sequences in core_nt. Its contigs are then called from related genera, and the call can differ between settings.
    • Mitochondrion screen. The coverage of a multi-block match is that of its best block only, as explained above.
    • Size of the BLAST table. At 50/5 the table holds several times more lines than at 10/1, but it stays a small text file.
    • Run time. It does not change in practice, because BLAST computes the alignments either way.

    The genus calls matter only with a genus filter, decontamination.mode include or exclude. With off, the default, every contig is kept whatever its genus, so the genus calls change no delivered genome file. See Decontamination.

    Notices and re-runs¶

    Both values must be whole numbers of at least 1. With the screen on, two notices can print when the workflow starts:

    [FunFlux] NOTE: parameters.blast_max_hsps is 1. Below 5, BLAST reports the coverage of a multi-block mitochondrial match as that of its best block only, which weakens the mitochondrion screen (a complete mitogenome can fall under min_direct_blast_qcov).
[FunFlux] NOTE: parameters.blast_max_target_seqs is 3; BLAST recommends 5 or more.

    The first notice, on blast_max_hsps, prints when that value is below 5 and mitochondria.mode is not off. The second, on blast_max_target_seqs, prints when that value is below 5.

    Changing either value runs the BLAST search again.

    Reproducing FunFlux 1.1.x¶

    Set these values only to reproduce results of FunFlux 1.1.x:

    parameters:
  blast_max_target_seqs: 10
  blast_max_hsps: 1

    The run then prints the blast_max_hsps notice, because the mitochondrion screen is weaker with one HSP. The 10 / 1 setting has not been run with v2 yet.
