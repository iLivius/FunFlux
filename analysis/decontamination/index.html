    Decontamination¶

    The contamination and mitochondrion screen, called the screen on this page, examines the draft assembly. This page covers its contamination part, which gives every contig of the draft a genus and keeps or removes contigs by genus. The screen runs before QC and annotation. A contaminant contig left in the assembly changes the QUAST, BUSCO and ITS results, and the fungal gene predictor calls false genes on it.

    The genus comes from a megablast search against NCBI core_nt. BlobTools turns the hits into one genus per contig, and the contig selector applies parameters.decontamination, which decides what is kept. The default, mode: off, removes nothing and still records a genus and a decision for every contig, so you can read them before you choose a filter. The mitochondrion screen reads the same BLAST results.

    Terms used on this page
    the screen the contamination and mitochondrion screen of the draft: BLAST against core_nt, BlobTools, the mitochondrion screen and the contig selector
    draft assembly (the draft) the assembly the screen examines, before any contig is removed: contigs_filt.fasta in illumina mode and in contigs mode with the screen, assembly.fasta in longread and hybrid
    read-based modes the modes illumina, longread and hybrid, which start from reads, as against contigs
    coverage track the reads aligned back to the draft, contaminants/{sample}_map.bam, from which BlobTools takes each contig's coverage and the contig summary its read depth (contig_depth, read-based modes); in contigs mode, the contigs aligned to themselves
    contig selector rule select_contigs: it keeps or removes each contig by genus, as parameters.decontamination says; the default, off, keeps every contig
    genus filter the setting parameters.decontamination.mode: include or exclude, under which the contig selector keeps or removes contigs by the genus BlobTools gave them
    confirmed mitochondrial contig a contig the mitochondrion screen classes confirmed_mitochondrial; with the default separate_confirmed, it is kept whatever its genus and moved to contigs_mitochondrial.fasta
    --forcerun a Snakemake option that runs the named rule again for every sample, with every step after it, even when Snakemake sees no change

    When the screen runs¶

    The screen always runs in the read-based modes, and in contigs mode only on request. It works the same in every mode, except for the coverage track (below).

    Mode Screen Screened draft, in 02.assembly/{sample}/ Kept contigs then go through
    contigs with parameters.contigs.screen: true contigs_filt.fasta: the input FASTA after the contig filter nothing more
    illumina always contigs_filt.fasta: the SPAdes contigs after the length and coverage filter nothing more
    longread always assembly.fasta: the hifiasm or Flye assembly Medaka (ONT samples)
    hybrid always assembly.fasta: the hifiasm or Flye assembly Medaka (ONT samples), then Polypolish
    • After the screen, the kept contigs, polished where the table says so, become contigs_all.fasta. In every mode that file is then split into the nuclear genome contigs_final.fasta and contigs_mitochondrial.fasta, as described in Mitochondria.
    • In hybrid, the Illumina guide assembly gets a screen of its own, with the same parameters.decontamination and parameters.mitochondria settings. Its steps carry guide in their names, from index_guide_contigs to select_guide_contigs, and its files are under 02.assembly/{sample}/guide/. The read pairs that map to its kept contigs, the selected pairs, go to Polypolish and, for ONT samples with short_read_guidance: true (off by default), to Filtlong (Hybrid). A contig removed from the guide therefore also loses its Illumina reads for those steps.
    • With the screen on, directories.blast_db must be set, and an empty value stops the run before any job starts. In contigs mode without the screen, the dry run prints a NOTE that the FASTA gets no contamination or mitochondrion screen (Pre-assembled contigs).

    Steps and files¶

    Two steps gather the evidence, map_contigs and blast_contigs. Two BlobTools steps join it into one row per contig, and the contig selector, select_contigs, decides. In the read-based modes, contig_depth also measures the read depth of every contig, for the contig summary.

    draft contigs
 ├── map_contigs ─────┐                                  coverage track (BAM)
 │                    ├── blob_json ── blob_table ──┐    one taxonomy row per contig
 ├── blast_contigs ───┘                             │
 │        └── screen_mitochondria ── confirmed IDs ─┤    see Mitochondria
 └──────────────────────────────────────────────────┴── select_contigs ── contigs_decontam.fasta
                                                                          + audit files
    Step Tool Does Writes, in 02.assembly/{sample}/contaminants/
    map_contigs contigs: minimap2 2.30, samtools 1.21; illumina: bowtie2 2.5.4 (index built by index_contigs), samtools 1.21; longread, hybrid: minimap2 2.30 with the sample's preset, samtools 1.21 aligns the reads to the draft: the coverage track {sample}_map.bam, {sample}_map.bam.bai
    blast_contigs BLAST+ 2.16.0 searches every contig in core_nt blastout
    blob_json BlobTools 1.1.1 joins the BLAST hits and the coverage blob.blobDB.json, blob.{sample}_map.bam.cov
    blob_table BlobTools 1.1.1 gives each contig one taxon per rank bestscore.blob.blobDB.table.txt
    select_contigs select_contigs_by_taxonomy.py (Python standard library), with the pinned Python 3.12 of the python conda environment keeps or removes each contig by its genus contig_taxonomy_decisions.tsv, {sample}_composition.txt, contigs.list, contigs_decontam.fasta
    contig_depth samtools 1.21 read-based modes only: measures the mean read depth of every contig from the coverage track, for the contig summary; the screen does not use it {sample}_depth.tsv

    The step names are those Snakemake prints on the console, and each step writes logs/<step>_{sample}.log. BLAST uses at most 24 threads and the mapping resources.threads, while every other step uses one. Output files lists which files stay after the run.

    Choosing a policy¶

    Start with off, read the decisions, then filter. off is the default and removes nothing, while exclude with a genus that core_nt gives to your own contigs removes genome. The table suggests a setting for each situation.

    Situation Setting
    first run of any isolate mode: off; read contig_taxonomy_decisions.tsv and the composition file (Audit files)
    a contaminant genus (a bacterium or yeast grown with the culture) has contigs of its own, and core_nt does not give that genus to your fungus mode: exclude with that genus
    contig_taxonomy_decisions.tsv shows which genus labels your isolate's contigs carry, and every other label belongs to a contaminant mode: include with the labels of your isolate's contigs, which need not include its true genus
    one sample of a batch needs another policy a sample_overrides row for that sample
    only the contigs without any BLAST hit should go mode: exclude with no genus listed, and discard_no_hit: true

    The screen can run alone, so that you see the decisions of a policy before QC and annotation start:

    snakemake --sdm conda --cores 24 --configfile config/config_custom.yaml --until select_contigs

    The keep/drop decision¶

    The contig selector takes the genus of each contig from the BlobTools table (BlobTools) and applies parameters.decontamination:

    parameters:
  decontamination:
    mode: off                  # off | include | exclude
    discard_no_hit: false
    include_genera:
    include_genera_by_sample:
    exclude_genera:
    exclude_genera_file:
    sample_overrides:

    The dry run prints the policy in use, for example Decontamination mode: off (discard_no_hit=false).

    Modes¶

    parameters.decontamination.mode, written mode on this page, chooses one of three policies.

    mode Keeps Removes
    off (default) every contig nothing
    include the contigs of the listed genera every other contig
    exclude every contig whose genus is not listed the contigs of the listed genera

    For each contig the tests run in this order, and the first that applies decides.

    1. A confirmed mitochondrial contig is kept (Protected mitochondrial contigs).
    2. With mode: off, the contig is kept.
    3. With discard_no_hit: true, a contig whose genus is no-hit is removed. This applies in include and exclude alike.
    4. Otherwise the genus test of the mode decides.

    include must list the genera that BLAST gave the isolate's own contigs, which for a poorly sequenced fungus need not be its true genus (Why there is no auto mode). When the screen runs, a sample left in include mode without any genus stops the run before any job starts:

    [FunFlux] parameters.decontamination: mode include keeps only the genera you name, but no genus is given for sample(s) S. Name the genus in include_genera, in include_genera_by_sample (one row per sample) or in the include_genera column of sample_overrides.

    discard_no_hit accepts true/false, yes/no, on/off or 1/0. It changes which contigs are kept only in exclude mode.

    mode Effect of discard_no_hit: true
    off none
    include only the reason code of the no-hit contigs changes (discarded_no_hit); they go anyway because they are not on the list
    exclude the no-hit contigs are removed

    Genus lists and aliases¶

    include_genera and exclude_genera take one genus, a YAML list, or one string with the names separated by ; or ,:

        include_genera: [Botrytis, Alternaria]
    exclude_genera: "Pseudomonas;Bacillus"

    Genus names are compared without regard to case, and exclude uses no aliases. include also accepts two kinds of alias, which only ever keep more contigs.

    • A table of bacterial genus synonyms, under which Peribacillus and Priestia match Bacillus. It contains no fungal genus.
    • The prefix rule, which strips the prefixes brady, meso, neo, sino, aeri, caldi and geo when at least four letters remain. Neocosmospora and Cosmospora then match each other, and so do Geosmithia and Smithia.

    For a fungus, only the prefix rule can apply.

    Per-sample settings¶

    Three settings name files. The two genus files, include_genera_by_sample and exclude_genera_file, add to the run-wide lists, and a sample_overrides row has the last word. When the screen runs, the dry run checks the files before any job starts, as described below, and select_contigs reads them again when it runs. A relative path is resolved from the launch folder, the folder Snakemake is started from, so write absolute paths.

    Key File Combines with the run-wide settings
    include_genera_by_sample TSV with the header columns sample and genus; several rows per sample allowed; a cell may hold A;B adds the genera to include_genera for that sample
    exclude_genera_file text file, one or more genera per line, separated by ; or , (not by spaces); lines that start with # are skipped, and a # after a genus is read as part of the name adds the genera to exclude_genera for every sample
    sample_overrides TSV with the header columns sample and mode (required) and include_genera, exclude_genera, discard_no_hit (optional) replaces, for that sample, each setting the row fills in

    The run-wide lists come first, the genera of the two genus files are added to them, and then the sample_overrides row is applied. An override's include_genera therefore replaces both the run-wide list and the include_genera_by_sample additions of that sample.

    In the example override table below, which is tab-separated, isolate1 keeps every contig and isolate2 loses its Pseudomonas contigs and its contigs without hits. Every other sample follows the run-wide policy.

    sample  mode    include_genera  exclude_genera  discard_no_hit
isolate1    off         
isolate2    exclude     Pseudomonas true

    An empty cell keeps the run-wide value. Every row needs all its tabs, including those of empty cells, because a missing tab shifts the columns.

    When the screen runs, a faulty file stops the run before any job starts. The dry run checks the three files against the samples of the run and stops in these cases.

    Checked Stops the run when
    each file that is set it is not an existing file, or is not UTF-8 text
    the header line include_genera_by_sample lacks the columns sample and genus, or sample_overrides lacks sample and mode
    the mode of every sample_overrides row, whatever its sample it is not off, include, exclude or blank (auto gets its own message, with the explanation quoted under Why there is no auto mode)
    the discard_no_hit cell of a sample of the run it is not true/false, yes/no, on/off, 1/0 or blank
    the genera of a sample of the run the sample is left in include mode without a genus (the message above)

    The messages of the first four checks name the key and the file and, for a bad row, its sample, for example [FunFlux] parameters.decontamination.sample_overrides: sample 'S' has mode 'M' in '<table>'; the mode column must be off, include, exclude or blank (blank keeps the run-wide mode). Without the screen, the files are not read and not checked. A table edited after the dry run is checked again when select_contigs runs, and a faulty one then stops that step, with a Python error in logs/select_contigs_{sample}.log.

    A configuration that still has the FunFlux 1.x key parameters.genus and no decontamination block is translated, with a NOTE, as described in Coming from v1. A genus in the v1 key becomes mode: include with that genus, and the NOTE says that include keeps only the contigs BLAST assigned to it. An empty v1 key becomes mode: off, and the NOTE says that FunFlux 1.x then kept the genus with the most contigs, and that v2 makes no automatic choice:

    [FunFlux] NOTE: parameters.genus (a v1 key) is empty. FunFlux 1.x kept the genus with the most contigs when parameters.genus was empty; FunFlux v2 has no automatic choice, so every contig is kept (decontamination mode off). To filter, read contig_taxonomy_decisions.tsv, then set parameters.decontamination: exclude the contaminant genera, or include the genera that BLAST assigned to the isolate's own contigs.

    Protected mitochondrial contigs¶

    The contig selector also reads mitochondria/confirmed_mitochondrial.ids, the contigs that screen_mitochondria confirmed from the same BLAST table. It keeps them before any other test, mode: off and discard_no_hit included, with the reason protected_confirmed_mitochondrial.

    The protection is needed because BLAST can give a fungal mitochondrion a genus other than the one a genus filter keeps, or no hit at all. The nuclear contigs of a poorly sequenced fungus, for example, can be labelled with a better-sequenced relative, while its mitochondrion, whose reference genome is in core_nt, carries the true genus. Without the protection, include with the relative alone would then remove the mitochondrion.

    A contig whose best mitochondrial hit is an animal's or a plant's, such as a short contig of human mitochondrial DNA from the library, is not confirmed (class foreign_mitochondrial) and so not protected. The contig selector treats it like any other contig: with the default mode: off it is kept, and with a genus filter its genus decides (Mitochondria).

    A protected contig that has no BlobTools row is kept with the genus no-taxonomy. A protected ID that is not in the draft FASTA stops the step (Protected mitochondrial contig(s) were not found in FASTA: ...), which means that the list and the FASTA come from different assemblies.

    Only the default mitochondria.mode: separate_confirmed protects the mitochondrion from a genus filter

    With mitochondria.mode: report or off, the list of confirmed contigs is empty and nothing is protected. A contig that the contig selector removes cannot come back at the later split.

    Why there is no auto mode¶

    FunFlux 1.x and BacFlux, the sister workflow for bacteria, have a fourth mode, auto, which keeps one dominant genus and removes every other contig. FunFlux 1.x and BacFlux up to v2.0.1 chose the genus that BlobTools gives to the most contigs. BacFlux v2.1.0 by default chooses the genus that holds the most bases (auto_vote: bp). FunFlux v2 has no auto mode, because BLAST genus labels are unreliable for poorly sequenced fungi.

    When core_nt holds few sequences of the isolate's genus, BlobTools gives most of the isolate's contigs the genus of better-sequenced relatives, often several of them, and many short contigs can get no hit at all. In FunFlux 1.x, auto counted contigs, not bases, and the no-hit contigs took part in the vote unless discard_no_hit: true. The winner could therefore be no-hit or a mislabel, and every contig with another label, genuine genome included, was removed.

    auto in parameters.decontamination.mode stops every run before any job starts:

    [FunFlux] parameters.decontamination.mode: 'auto' is not available in FunFlux. BLAST genus labels are unreliable for poorly sequenced fungi, so auto can remove most of the genome. Use off (the default), read contig_taxonomy_decisions.tsv, then exclude the contaminant genera or include the genera that BLAST assigned to the isolate's own contigs.

    In the mode column of sample_overrides, auto stops the run with the same explanation, but only when the screen runs, because without the screen that table is not read.

    Removal by genus removes genome when the genus calls are wrong

    include and exclude remain, because there you name the genera after reading contig_taxonomy_decisions.tsv and the composition file. The trap is the true genus. When core_nt holds few of its sequences, BLAST gives it to only a few of the isolate's contigs, and include with the true genus alone removes most of the genome. The warnings in logs/select_contigs_{sample}.log flag such a removal, but with include or exclude the run then goes on to annotate what is left.

    The calls also depend on the BLAST hit depth. Keeping more hits per contig changes the bit-score sums, and with them the genus of some contigs (BLAST hit depth). By default FunFlux 2.1.0 keeps 10 database sequences per contig, where FunFlux 2.0.0 kept 50, so a genus list chosen from a 2.0.0 run may no longer fit: read the new contig_taxonomy_decisions.tsv before you filter again. A configuration copied from the 2.0.0 template still sets 50 (Updating from FunFlux 2.0.0).

    Audit files¶

    The contig selector records its decision on every contig in one table, the share of each genus in a composition file, and its warnings in a log.

    contig_taxonomy_decisions.tsv¶

    The decisions table has one row per contig of the BlobTools table, in table order, plus any protected contig without a BlobTools row.

    Column Content
    contig contig name, the first word of the FASTA header
    assigned_genus the BlobTools genus, no-hit, or no-taxonomy
    action keep or remove
    reason one of the codes below

    The reason column holds one of these codes.

    reason Meaning
    protected_confirmed_mitochondrial confirmed mitochondrial contig, kept in every mode
    mode_off mode: off
    discarded_no_hit no BLAST hit, and discard_no_hit: true
    included_genus include; genus listed
    included_genus_alias include; an alias of a listed genus
    not_included_genus include; genus not listed
    excluded_genus exclude; genus listed
    not_excluded_genus exclude; genus not listed

    With the default settings, every row reads keep / mode_off, except those of the confirmed mitochondrial contigs, which read keep / protected_confirmed_mitochondrial.

    Composition file¶

    {sample}_composition.txt has one line per genus, over every contig of the BlobTools table, kept or removed. Each line gives the genus share of the bases and of the contig count, as fractions with two decimals, and the lines are sorted by bases:

    <genus>: bases <share of the bases>  contigs <share of the contigs>

    The two shares can disagree, because a genus can hold many short contigs while another holds most of the genome.

    Log messages¶

    The contig selector writes these messages to logs/select_contigs_{sample}.log.

    Message Printed when
    WARNING: N genera each hold at least 5% of this assembly (...). Either the sample is a mixed culture, or one genome is being split across related genera by the BLAST assignment. Check the kept/removed split in contig_taxonomy_decisions.tsv before trusting this assembly. two or more genera hold at least 5 % of the bases
    WARNING: decontamination is discarding X% of the assembly (R of T bp). If the kept assembly then looks incomplete but NOT contaminated, the filter has most likely removed genome rather than contamination. more than 20 % of the bases are removed
    WARNING: N contigs from BlobTools table were not found in FASTA. the BlobTools table names contigs the FASTA lacks
    Sample S decontamination mode: M and Kept K of N contig assignments. when the step succeeds, as the last two lines

    The warnings change no decision and do not stop the run. They are checked in every mode, so the first one can appear with the default mode: off too.

    The step fails in these cases.

    The step fails when Message
    no contig is kept No contigs were kept for sample 'S' with mode 'M'. Check taxonomy assignments and decontamination settings.
    a protected ID is missing from the FASTA Protected mitochondrial contig(s) were not found in FASTA: ...
    include has no genus (the dry run normally stops that case first) Mode 'include' requires at least one genus.
    the BlobTools table has no readable row No contig taxonomy records could be parsed from '<table>'.

    Snakemake then deletes the outputs of the step, the decisions table included. Add --keep-incomplete to the command line to keep them for inspection. Snakemake treats files kept this way as finished, so delete them, or add --forcerun select_contigs, before the next run.

    Changing the policy after a run¶

    Snakemake notices a changed decontamination setting, BLAST hit depth or mitochondrion threshold, and re-runs the steps that use it and every step after them.

    Change Re-runs
    a new decontamination policy select_contigs, the mitochondrion split, QC, ITS and the whole annotation of every sample: hours per sample
    a new hit depth, nt_version or blast_db path BLAST as well (in hybrid also the guide's BLAST), and every step after it
    a new mitochondrion threshold the mitochondrion screen and every step after it

    A BLAST database or a file-setting table changed in place needs --forcerun

    A changed setting re-runs everything after it by itself. A BLAST database updated in place re-runs nothing, so add --forcerun blast_contigs. A table of the three file settings edited in place re-runs nothing either, because Snakemake sees the path, not the content. Add --forcerun select_contigs to apply the edited table.

    Coverage track¶

    blobtools create requires a BAM file of reads aligned to the draft. Only the read-based modes give a real coverage track.

    Mode Reads aligned to the draft Aligner BAM after the run
    contigs none: the contigs are aligned to themselves minimap2 -a, no preset deleted once BlobTools has read it
    illumina the trimmed read pairs bowtie2 kept; Qualimap and contig_depth read it
    longread, hybrid the filtered long reads minimap2, map-ont or map-hifi per sample kept; Qualimap and contig_depth read it

    In the read-based modes, bowtie2 runs in its default end-to-end mode and minimap2 with the sample's preset. Qualimap's report on the kept BAM is described on Assembly QC. From the same BAM, contig_depth measures the mean read depth of every contig with samtools coverage, for the contig summary, because the coverage column of the BlobTools table is an estimate that can run low on long reads.

    In contigs mode the coverage values mean nothing

    In contigs mode the BAM exists because BlobTools needs one. Each contig is one "read" that maps once, to itself, and BlobTools computes coverage as mapped reads × mean read length / contig length. The value is therefore the mean contig length divided by the contig's own length, below 1 for contigs longer than the mean and above 1 for shorter ones. The contig selector reads only the genus, in every mode.

    BLAST against core_nt¶

    blast_contigs runs one megablast search per sample, here with the default settings:

    BLASTDB=/path/to/blast_db \
blastn -task megablast \
  -query 02.assembly/{sample}/contigs_filt.fasta \
  -db /path/to/blast_db/core_nt \
  -outfmt '6 qseqid staxids bitscore pident evalue length qlen slen qcovs qcovhsp sskingdoms scomnames sscinames sblastnames stitle' \
  -num_threads 24 -evalue 1e-10 \
  -max_target_seqs 10 -max_hsps 5 \
  -out 02.assembly/{sample}/contaminants/blastout

    BLAST's megablast task looks for highly similar sequences and is much faster than the more sensitive blastn task. directories.blast_db must hold the files below.

    File Read by If missing
    the BLAST database named by parameters.nt_version (default core_nt) blastn BLAST fails
    taxdb.btd, taxdb.bti blastn, for the organism-name columns those columns read N/A
    nodes.dmp, names.dmp (NCBI taxdump) BlobTools; nodes.dmp also the mitochondrion screen, for the organism of the best mitochondrial hit the dry run stops with MissingInputException in rule blob_json (blob_guide_json in hybrid mode)

    Without taxdb the run goes on, with N/A for the organism names. The downloads are described on Reference databases.

    parameters.nt_version (default core_nt) is the name of the database inside directories.blast_db, either core_nt or nt. Two more settings decide how much BLAST keeps per contig.

    Key BLAST option Keeps Default Allowed The dry run prints a NOTE when
    parameters.blast_max_target_seqs -max_target_seqs database sequences ("subjects") per contig 10 integer ≥ 1 below 5
    parameters.blast_max_hsps -max_hsps alignments per subject 5 integer ≥ 1 below 5, unless mitochondria.mode is off

    Keep the defaults, 10 subjects per contig with 5 alignments each, unless the mitochondrion is not confirmed (Mitochondria): much smaller values lose evidence, and much larger ones skew the genus calls.

    • BLAST applies -max_target_seqs during the search, not as a final best-N filter, so a small value can lose better matches. BLAST recommends 5 or more.
    • A large -max_target_seqs skews the genus calls. BlobTools adds up the bit scores of every kept hit per genus, so a genus with many core_nt entries of one gene (the same gene sequenced from many isolates) can outvote the genus of the closest matches.
    • The qcovs column (query coverage per subject), which the mitochondrion screen reads, is computed from the kept alignments only. With -max_hsps 1, BLAST keeps only the single best local alignment per database sequence, so a complete mitochondrial genome that aligns in several blocks can look only half covered.

    The evidence behind these defaults, and the effect of the hit depth on the genus calls, are described on BLAST hit depth.

    BLAST is the slow step of the screen. On 24 threads it took 33 to 51 minutes for the 42.6 Mb Botrytis cinerea B05.10 assemblies of the validation runs. In contigs mode, every other step of the screen takes seconds.

    BlobTools: one genus per contig¶

    BlobTools joins the BLAST hits and the coverage track into one table, with a taxon per contig and rank. blob_json runs blobtools create on the draft, the BAM, blastout, nodes.dmp and names.dmp, and blob_table flattens the result:

    blobtools view --input blob.blobDB.json --out bestscore --taxrule bestsum --rank all --hits

    bestsum adds up the bit scores of all hits per taxon, separately at each rank, and the taxon with the highest sum wins. bestscore.blob.blobDB.table.txt has one row per contig, with these columns.

    Columns Content
    1 to 5 name, length, GC, N count and coverage (bam0)
    6 to 29, four per rank: superkingdom, phylum, order, family, genus and species the winning taxon, its summed score, the number of other taxa with hits and, added by --hits, every competing taxon with its own sum

    The contig selector reads only the genus, column 22, and a contig without a BLAST hit has the genus no-hit. The competing taxa show how close a call was.

    Output files¶

    The steps on this page write these files to 02.assembly/{sample}/contaminants/. Everything the decision rests on is kept. Only the BlobTools intermediates and, in contigs mode, the BAM are deleted.

    File Kept Content
    blastout yes megablast hits, one line per alignment, the 15 columns of -outfmt above
    bestscore.blob.blobDB.table.txt yes BlobTools table, one row per contig
    contig_taxonomy_decisions.tsv yes genus, action and reason per contig
    {sample}_composition.txt yes share of bases and contigs per genus
    contigs.list yes names of the kept contigs
