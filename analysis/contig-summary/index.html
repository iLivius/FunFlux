    Contig summary¶

    A fungal assembly is a list of contigs, and the list alone does not say what each contig is. For every sample, FunFlux writes one table that does: 02.assembly/{sample}/eval/contig_summary.tsv. It has one row per contig of the draft assembly, with the evidence the run produced about that contig and a verdict with its reasons. The table also appears in the MultiQC report. Read this page to interpret the verdicts, to see how the telomere repeat unit is chosen, and to set the unit yourself when the data cannot choose it.

    Terms used on this page
    draft assembly (the draft) the assembly before the screen removes any contig, such as contigs_filt.fasta or assembly.fasta; the contig summary describes it
    the screen the contamination and mitochondrion screen of the draft: BLAST against core_nt, BlobTools, the mitochondrion screen and the contig selector; in contigs mode only with parameters.contigs.screen: true
    verdict the label the contig summary gives each draft contig, one of ten such as chromosome, chromosome_arm, rDNA_array or mitochondrion, with its reasons
    telomere repeat unit (telomere unit) the short sequence repeated in tandem at chromosome ends, TTAGGG in most filamentous fungi; FunFlux chooses it from each assembly unless parameters.contig_summary.telomere_motif names one
    telomere array tandem copies of the telomere repeat unit at a contig end; by default a telomere needs 5 or more copies within 25,000 bp of the end
    canonical form the spelling tidk gives a repeat unit: the alphabetically first of its rotations and those of its reverse complement, so TTAGGG is written AACCCT
    rDNA unit one copy of the repeat SSU-ITS1-5.8S-ITS2-LSU; FunFlux counts one per 5.8S gene found, that is, one per ITS window
    BUSCO marker a BUSCO gene found complete on a contig (Complete or Duplicated); the contig summary counts them per contig as evidence of nuclear sequence

    What the contigs of a fungal assembly are¶

    A long-read assembly of a filamentous fungus holds a few kinds of contig side by side, and a short-read assembly adds two more kinds, by the hundred. Each kind has its own signature in the evidence FunFlux already produces, as the table below shows. Telomeres, the first piece of evidence, are tandem arrays of a short repeat unit that cap every chromosome end. The unit is TTAGGG in most filamentous fungi. The left end of a chromosome reads CCCTAA on the forward strand, and the right end reads TTAGGG.

    Kind of contig Telomere arrays Read depth Genes and BUSCO markers Other signs
    whole chromosome, telomere to telomere at both ends the depth of the bulk of the genome along its whole length size says nothing: an accessory chromosome or a minichromosome of 200 to 300 kb is a chromosome like a 4-Mb one
    chromosome arm at one end only; the other end is where the assembler stopped, usually at a repeat it could not resolve: the rDNA array, a centromere as a chromosome as a chromosome
    mitochondrion, a small genome (82 kb in Botrytis cinerea B05.10, the worked example) present in many copies per cell several times the nuclear depth a long-read assembler usually closes it into a circle and flags it as circular
    piece of the rDNA array can carry one at one end, when the array sits at a chromosome end says little: the reads of all the copies are shared out over however many pieces the assembler made of the array no BUSCO marker; few or no protein-coding genes several 5.8S genes in a row (the ITS windows of the taxonomy step, one per rDNA unit)
    alternative copy of a region, or noise: a second haplotype assembled on its own, a stretch assembled twice, or a leftover of the assembly graph well below the genome's, because the reads of the region were split between two copies, or were few
    contaminant: a contig from another organism its own, not the genome's its own GC content; the screen labelled it with a genus from its BLAST hits and removed it
    collapsed repeat (short-read assemblies) well above the genome's: the reads of all the copies pile up on it no BUSCO marker, at most a gene call or two (predictors call open reading frames inside transposons) several genome copies of a transposon, a satellite or another repeat family, assembled into one contig because short reads cannot tell the copies apart
    short piece of the nuclear genome (short-read assemblies) the depth of the genome none, because it is too short to carry a gene hundreds to a few thousand bases; nuclear sequence all the same, left over where the assembly broke

    An empty cell means that the evidence is not part of that kind's signature. Taken together, the columns sort the contigs. Telomeres tell a chromosome from an arm. Depth sets the mitochondrion, collapsed repeats and alternative copies apart from the nuclear genome. Genes and BUSCO markers tell nuclear sequence from rDNA and debris. A few points the table cannot hold:

    • With the default settings, the mitochondrion screen confirms the mitochondrion from a BLAST hit to a mitochondrial genome that covers at least 35 % of the contig. In contigs and illumina mode, and on the Illumina guide assembly of hybrid mode, that hit is enough. On the long-read assembly of longread and hybrid mode, the hit must also be backed by the assembler's evidence, unless it covers 70 % or more. That support is an assembler depth of at least 3 times the median of all contigs, or a clean circle: a contig recorded as circular, not a repeat and in no alternative group. By default, the confirmed mitochondrion is then split off before the annotation. See Mitochondria.
    • The ribosomal RNA genes sit in tens to hundreds of tandem copies of one unit, SSU-ITS1-5.8S-ITS2-LSU, that no assembler resolves whole.
    • A long-read assembler resolves most repeats in place, so a long-read assembly has few collapsed repeats.

    The contig summary gathers the evidence behind these signatures into one row per contig, and says for each contig how it was weighed. The telomere arrays are found with tidk, in the sequence itself. The other evidence is the read depth, the assembler's depth and circularity flag, the rDNA units, the BUSCO markers, the predicted genes, and the calls of the contamination and mitochondrion screen.

    The table¶

    02.assembly/{sample}/eval/contig_summary.tsv has one row per contig of the draft assembly. Its verdict and reason columns give the answer, annotated_as gives the contig's name in the annotation, and the other columns hold the evidence:

    Column Content NA when
    contig the name in the draft assembly: the first word of the FASTA header, as the screen tables use it never
    length bp never
    gc GC fraction, 3 decimals never
    depth read depth from the BlobTools table (bam0), 2 decimals contigs mode; a contig without a row in the table
    depth_x_median depth divided by the median depth of the nuclear genome, 2 decimals (the median) depth is NA
    assembler_depth the assembler's own depth, 1 decimal illumina and contigs mode; an empty cell in assembly_evidence.tsv
    circular Y or N, the assembler's flag illumina and contigs mode
    telomere_start, telomere_end copies of the telomere unit in the array at that end: the array length tidk measured, divided by the unit length and rounded; only an array that points the way a telomere points counts (Telomeres) never (0 when none)
    telomere_end_offset_start, telomere_end_offset_end distance (bp) from the contig end to the array no telomere array at that end
    telomere_unit the telomere repeat unit of the assembly in tidk's canonical form (AACCCT for TTAGGG), the same on every row of a sample; none when no unit was found never
    rdna_units ITS windows on the contig, that is, 5.8S genes found never (0 when none)
    busco_markers complete BUSCO markers on the contig (status Complete or Duplicated; a duplicated marker counts where each copy lies) never (0 when none)
    genes mRNA features of the annotation on the scaffold the contig became the contig has no scaffold in contigs_sort.fasta: dropped by funannotate clean, split off as the mitochondrion, or removed by the screen
    genus assigned_genus of contig_taxonomy_decisions.tsv no screen
    screen kept (<reason>), removed (<reason>) or mitochondrion (<reason>), with the reason of the contig selector or, for a mitochondrion, of the mitochondrion screen; a mitochondrial_candidate, circular_unclassified or high_coverage_unclassified class of the mitochondrion screen is appended after ; no screen
    annotated_as scaffold_N, the name of the contig in every annotation file; or dropped by funannotate clean, not annotated (mitochondrion), not annotated (removed by the screen) never
    verdict one of the ten verdicts (The verdict): mitochondrion, contaminant, rDNA_array, chromosome, chromosome_arm, low_depth, repeat, nuclear_contig, nuclear_fragment, unclassified never
    reason the evidence behind the verdict, in words, for example telomere repeats at both ends (17 and 19 copies); depth 1.00x the median; 543 genes, 131 BUSCO markers never

    The table describes the draft assembly, which is contigs_final.fasta only in contigs mode without the screen. Which file is the draft depends on the mode:

    Mode The draft
    illumina; contigs with the screen contigs_filt.fasta
    longread, hybrid assembly.fasta
    contigs without the screen contigs_final.fasta

    The contigs the screen removed and the mitochondrion have a row too

    Because the table describes the draft, the contigs the screen removed and the mitochondrion get a row too, and nothing disappears silently. Evidence a mode cannot produce is NA, and the verdict rests on the rest.

    The verdict¶

    Ten verdict rules give each contig its verdict. They are tried in the order of the table below, and the first that applies decides. Evidence a mode cannot produce is unknown (NA in the table) and is not tested, so "depth within 0.5 to 1.5 times the median when known" passes when the depth is unknown. A telomere is a contig end where tidk found an array of the telomere unit of at least min_telomere_copies copies, pointing the way a telomere points.

    Rule Verdict The contig has What it is
    1 mitochondrion the class confirmed_mitochondrial from the mitochondrion screen, or a place in contigs_mitochondrial.fasta the mitochondrial genome
    2 contaminant the action remove from the screen; the reason names the genus a contig of another organism
    3 rDNA_array at least parameters.contig_summary.min_rdna_units rDNA units (3 by default), and fewer predicted genes than rDNA units, or no gene count a piece of the rDNA array
    4 chromosome telomere repeats at both ends; depth within 0.5 to 1.5 times the median when known; at least one gene when known a whole chromosome; size plays no part, so a small accessory chromosome qualifies
    5 chromosome_arm telomere repeats at one end; the rest as in rule 4 a chromosome arm
    6 low_depth depth below 0.5 times the median an alternative copy of a region (a second haplotype, a stretch assembled twice) or noise
    7 repeat no BUSCO marker; at most two predicted genes; depth above 1.5 times the median (when the depth is unknown this rule does not fire) a collapsed repeat: several genome copies of a transposon, a satellite or another repeat family assembled into one contig, so the reads of all the copies pile up on it
    8 nuclear_contig at least one gene or BUSCO marker; depth within 0.5 to 1.5 times the median when known; no telomere repeat a fragment of a chromosome
    9 nuclear_fragment no gene and no BUSCO marker; no telomere repeat; depth within 0.5 to 1.5 times the median, or unknown a piece of the nuclear genome too short to carry a gene; a short-read assembly ends in many of them
    10 unclassified anything else the reason says what was and was not seen

    The screen decides first, then the rDNA count, then the telomeres, and depth and genes sort out the rest. Two verdict rules allow a gene call where none is expected. In verdict rule 3, a pure rDNA array holds no protein-coding gene, but the gene predictor now and then calls a gene inside an rDNA unit. A contig with more rDNA units than genes is still mostly rDNA. In verdict rule 7, a gene call or two is allowed because gene predictors call open reading frames inside transposons.

    "No BUSCO marker" (verdict rule 7) and "no gene and no BUSCO marker" (verdict rule 9) apply only when at least one of the two counts is known, and then every known count they name must be 0. In a run both are always counted, because BUSCO and funannotate run in every mode. Only a contig without a scaffold in contigs_sort.fasta lacks its gene count.

    The reason column spells out the evidence each verdict rule used, with the numbers, so a verdict can be checked against the columns.

    The median depth¶

    The median depth is what depth_x_median and the verdict rules compare each contig with. It is weighted by length, so a 4-Mb chromosome counts a hundred times as much as a 40-kb piece. It is taken over the contigs of at least 100 kb, or over all contigs when none is that long, and it leaves out the mitochondrion and the contigs the screen removed. Short debris, the deep mitochondrion and a contaminant genome at its own depth therefore cannot pull the median away from the depth of the bulk of the genome.

    How the table is made¶

    Where it runs¶

    Three steps make the table, in every mode. The telomere unit is chosen as soon as the draft exists, and the table itself waits for the annotation:

    Step Runs Does Writes
    telomere_db once per output folder downloads the tidk telomere repeat database and checks its SHA-256 (Reference databases) 00.resources/telomeres/telo_database_v2.tsv, PROVENANCE.txt
    telomere_units once per sample, as soon as the draft exists runs tidk on the draft and chooses the telomere repeat unit of the assembly (Telomeres); its own conda environment (tidk 0.3.0 with Python 3.12); seconds to a minute eval/telomere_candidates.tsv, telomere_unit.txt, telomere_ends.tsv
    contig_summary once per sample, after the annotation; one of the last jobs of a sample gathers the evidence into the table eval/contig_summary.tsv, contig_summary_mqc.tsv

    The MultiQC report reads the _mqc copy of the table, and no other step reads it.

    Where the evidence comes from¶

    Telomeres, rDNA units, BUSCO markers and genes are known in every mode. The read depth is known only in the read-based modes, the assembler's depth and circularity only in the long-read modes, and the calls of the screen only where the screen runs. Each piece of evidence is read from these files:

    Evidence What it says Taken from Modes
    telomere arrays at the contig ends at both ends: a whole chromosome; at one end: a chromosome arm eval/telomere_unit.txt and eval/telomere_ends.tsv: tidk on the draft sequence itself (Telomeres) all
    read depth the nuclear contigs share one depth; the mitochondrion is several times deeper; an alternative copy of a region, or noise, is much shallower contaminants/bestscore.blob.blobDB.table.txt, column bam0: the sample's reads on the draft (Coverage track) illumina, longread, hybrid
    assembler depth and circularity a circular contig at a high depth is the usual shape of a mitochondrion assembly_evidence.tsv, columns coverage and circular longread, hybrid
    rDNA units a contig made of rDNA units is a piece of the rDNA array, not a chromosome 03.taxonomy/{sample}/its_windows.tsv, one row per 5.8S gene found (ITS taxonomy) all
    BUSCO markers genuine nuclear sequence, as against rDNA and debris eval/busco/run_<lineage>/full_table.tsv of the specific lineage, the markers with status Complete or Duplicated (BUSCO) all
    predicted genes the same the mRNA features of 04.annotation/{sample}/funannotate/annotate_results/<Genus_species>_{sample}.gff3, joined to the draft contig through 04.annotation/{sample}/contigs_sort.fasta (the join) all
    the screen removed as a contaminant, with its genus; confirmed mitochondrial contaminants/contig_taxonomy_decisions.tsv, mitochondria/mitochondrial_screen.tsv, contigs_mitochondrial.fasta screen

    Paths without a stage prefix are under 02.assembly/{sample}/. In hybrid mode the screen files are those of the long-read draft, not of the Illumina guide.

    In contigs mode the screen maps the contigs to themselves, so the depth in the BlobTools table reflects contig length and nothing else (Coverage track). The contig summary therefore does not read it there, and the depth columns are NA in contigs mode, with or without the screen.

    Telomeres¶

    The telomere repeat unit differs between fungal groups (the table below), so FunFlux does not assume one. It chooses the unit from the assembly itself and says whether the unit it found is a known telomere repeat. Rule telomere_units runs tidk (Brown et al. 2025) on the draft assembly, and tidk does the heavy work. FunFlux uses two of its commands:

    tidk command What it does Used for
    tidk explore lists every tandem repeat unit of a sequence set with its copy number collecting the candidate units (step 2 below)
    tidk ends takes one unit and reports, for both ends of every contig, whether an array of that unit sits there, how long it is, how far from the contig end, and which way it points scoring each candidate (step 4) and the telomere columns of the table

    tidk measures the arrays, and FunFlux chooses the unit

    Neither command chooses the telomere unit of a genome. Most units tidk explore lists are microsatellites, and tidk ends needs the unit as input. The choice is FunFlux's, made from how each candidate unit behaves at the contig ends, and checked against the telomere repeat database that comes with tidk and against the literature.

    tidk writes every unit in a canonical form, the alphabetically smallest of all rotations of the unit and of its reverse complement, so TTAGGG, GGGTTA and CCCTAA are all AACCCT. The telomere_unit column, the three telomere files and the log use that form, and the table of database units under Which fungi carry which repeat gives the published spelling next to it.

    How the unit is chosen¶

    1. The contig ends. FunFlux writes a FASTA of terminal windows. Of every contig longer than two windows (parameters.contig_summary.telomere_window, 25,000 bp by default), it takes the first and the last window, as <contig>__start and <contig>__end. A contig of at most two windows goes in whole. The window is wide because the array is not always the last thing in a contig. When the assembler ran past the telomere, the array sits some kilobases inside the contig end, and a window that stops short of it misses the telomere altogether.

    2. The units found there. tidk explore lists every unit of 5 to 30 bp that forms a tandem run of at least min_telomere_copies copies in the windows, with its copy number (copies_at_ends). It runs once more on the whole draft (copies_genome_wide). A telomere unit has nearly all its copies at the ends, while a satellite has most of them inside the contigs. Both numbers go into the candidates table, but neither enters the score, because a microsatellite can have nearly as many copies at the contig ends as the telomere unit. The copy number also does not say which way its arrays point.

    3. The candidates. They are the units found at the ends, every unit the database lists with strong or weak support, every unit of the literature table and, when parameters.contig_summary.telomere_motif names one, that unit. All are written in canonical form and merged. Three filters drop what cannot be a telomere repeat, and the configured unit is exempt from them:

      Filter Drops, for example Why
      5 to 30 bp long ACAT, AAAT no telomere repeat shorter than 5 bp is known, and 4-bp units are low-complexity sequence that forms arrays everywhere: AAAT alone gives 192 "telomere" ends on the Illumina (SPAdes) assembly of Botrytis cinerea B05.10
      not a repeat of a shorter unit ACACAC (AC three times), TTAGGGTTAGGG the same array would be counted twice under two names
      the two strands differ: the unit holds more G than C, or more C than G AAAT, AGAGCC every telomerase-made repeat has a G-rich strand, and the orientation test of step 4 needs the two strands to be told apart

    4. The score. For every candidate, tidk ends runs on the whole draft. It searches the window at each contig end and accepts an array no further from the end than the window (-w and --max-offset, both telomere_window). An array must be at least min_telomere_copies copies long (--min-length, that is 30 bp for a 6-bp unit), and tidk's default mode tolerates sequencing errors inside the array. Each contig end gets a status:

      Status Meaning
      present an array pointing the way a telomere points: the C-rich strand at a contig start, the G-rich strand at a contig end
      wrong_strand an array pointing the other way
      not_terminal an array further from the end than the window
      absent no array
      other_end FunFlux's addition: on a contig shorter than two windows, one array is seen from both ends, once as present at its own end and once as wrong_strand from the other end; when the two rows describe the same stretch of the contig (their coordinates overlap), the row with the larger offset is relabelled and does not count

      Per unit, telomere_ends is the number of present rows, opposite_ends the number of wrong_strand rows, score their difference, and t2t_contigs the contigs with present at both ends. The reasoning is that a telomere repeat points one way at every chromosome end, with its G-rich strand towards the end. A satellite that happens to sit at contig ends points either way with equal chance, so however often it occurs, its score stays near zero.

    5. The choice. A unit is eligible with at least 2 telomere ends and more than twice as many telomere ends as opposite ends. The eligible units are ranked by score, then known units (literature or database) before unknown ones, then the shorter unit, then alphabetically. The first is the telomere unit of the assembly. When none is eligible, the unit is none, every telomere column reads 0, and a chromosome comes out as nuclear_contig. The log then names the best candidate and its counts.

    Which fungi carry which repeat¶

    TTAGGG, the vertebrate telomere repeat, is the unit of most filamentous Ascomycota (the Pezizomycotina), of most Basidiomycota and of the Mucoromycota. The departures are single species or small groups, and the budding yeasts, the fission yeasts and the Microstromatales have no single unit. The full references are on Citation and references. The table lists the known departures and the groups that no single unit describes:

    Fungi Telomere repeat Source
    most Pezizomycotina (filamentous Ascomycota), most Basidiomycota, Mucoromycota; among them Botrytis cinerea and Aspergillus fumigatus TTAGGG Lue 2021; Sun et al. 2023; Levis et al. 1997; Bowyer et al. 2022
    the Aspergillus flavus group TTAGGGTCAACA Sun et al. 2023
    Peltaster fructicola TAGGG Sun et al. 2023
    Drechmeria coniospora CCGTTGCTGTTG Sun et al. 2023
    Penicillium polonicum TTTAGGG Sun et al. 2023
    a few Basidiomycota: Malassezia, Hericium TTAGGA (Malassezia furfur), TTAGTG (Malassezia restricta, M. globosa), TTGGA (Hericium erinaceus) Sun et al. 2023
    Cryptococcus neoformans TTA(G)4-6: TTA followed by four to six G, a unit of varying length Edman 1992
    budding yeasts (Saccharomycotina): Candida albicans (23 bp), Kluyveromyces lactis (25 bp) and most others long and often irregular units of 8 to 25 bp; no single unit McEachern and Blackburn 1994; Červenák et al. 2021; Lue 2021
    Yarrowia lipolytica TTAGTCAGGG, a regular unit among the budding yeasts Lue 2021; Červenák et al. 2021
    fission yeasts (Schizosaccharomyces) degenerate GGTTAC-type repeats; no single unit Sepsiova et al. 2016
    Microstromatales yeasts no telomerase; a different tandem array at every chromosome end Brejová et al. 2026

    Every exact unit of this table is a candidate in every run. When the data choose one of them, the candidates table marks it in_literature. The telomere repeat database of tidk (Reference databases) adds the units reported with strong or weak support in its 2,990 rows of all kingdoms, 256 distinct units. A unit the data choose is marked in_database, with the number of species per kingdom that carry it. The 126 fungal rows of the database name these units with strong or weak support:

    Unit, canonical form Fungal species in the database
    AACCCT (TTAGGG) 20
    AAACCCT (TTTAGGG) 18
    AACCT (TTAGG) 9
    AACCTAACT 3
    AAAAAACCCT 2
    AAAACCCT, AACCCCT, AACACT, AACCC, AACCCTAT, AAAT 1 each

    TTAGGG and TTTAGGG account for most fungal species in the database. AAAT is 4 bp long and has no G-rich strand, so it fails two of the three filters and is never scored. For a species in none of these lists, TeloBase (Lyčka et al. 2024) and the Telomerase Database (Podlevsky et al. 2008) list the known telomere repeats.

    Setting the unit¶

    parameters.contig_summary.telomere_motif sets the telomere unit by hand. A configured unit is always used, and the data's own choice is still reported next to it.

    parameters:
  contig_summary:
    telomere_motif: auto             # or a unit, such as TTAGGGTCAACA for the Aspergillus flavus group
    telomere_motif Unit used The three filters Marked chosen in the candidates table
    auto (default) the unit chosen from the data, as described above applied the data's choice
    a unit: letters A, C, G and T only, 4 to 30 long, upper or lower case the unit as given, whatever its score: it is written to telomere_unit.txt and used for the telomere columns skipped the given unit

    Either strand and any rotation work, because tidk ends gives the same result for TTAGGG, CCCTAA and GGGTTA. The ranking still runs, and a NOTE says when the data favour another unit or give the configured one no support (the NOTE). Set a unit when the data cannot decide, for example in a fragmented assembly with few chromosome ends. A changed value of this key re-runs telomere_units, contig_summary and MultiQC.

    Setting one telomere_motif key does not set the other

    hifiasm has a key of the same name, which is separate:

    Key Read by The unit is written
    parameters.contig_summary.telomere_motif the contig summary on either strand, in any rotation
    parameters.longread.hifiasm.telomere_motif or parameters.hybrid.hifiasm.telomere_motif (--telo-m) the assembler, hifiasm as it reads at the 5' end of a chromosome: CCCTAA for TTAGGG

    The three telomere files¶

    Rule telomere_units writes three files to 02.assembly/{sample}/eval/. They hold the numbers behind the choice of the unit, and the arrays of that unit at every contig end.

    telomere_candidates.tsv has one row per unit that was found at the contig ends or formed at least one array when it was scored. A known unit that formed none is counted in the log only. The rows are sorted by score, then telomere_ends, both descending, then unit:

    Column Content
    unit the unit in canonical form
    unit_length bp
    copies_at_ends copies tidk explore counted in the terminal windows; 0 for a known unit that was not found there
    copies_genome_wide copies tidk explore counted on the whole draft
    telomere_ends contig ends with an array pointing the way a telomere points (present)
    opposite_ends contig ends with an array pointing the other way (wrong_strand)
    score telomere_ends minus opposite_ends
    t2t_contigs contigs with a present array at both ends
    in_literature the fungal groups or species of the literature table that carry the unit, with the published spelling and the references; - when none
    in_database species per kingdom with strong or weak support in the tidk database, for example Fungi 20; Metazoa 420; Viridiplantae 11; (no kingdom) 2 (the few rows without a kingdom are counted as (no kingdom)); - when none
    chosen yes for the telomere unit of the assembly, no otherwise

    telomere_unit.txt holds one line, the unit in canonical form or none.

    telomere_ends.tsv is the per-end table of the chosen unit after the same-array rule, the relabelling to other_end in step 4. It has tidk's ten columns and values, and holds the header line alone when the unit is none:

    Column Content
    id the contig
    sequence_length its length in bp
    side start or end
    status present, wrong_strand, not_terminal, absent or other_end (step 4)
    telomere_start, telomere_end the array's coordinates on the contig, 0-based; NA when absent
    telomere_length the array's length in bp
    offset distance from the contig end to the array, bp
    strand C-rich or G-rich; a telomere is C-rich at a contig start and G-rich at a contig end
    window_limited tidk's flag: true when the array reaches the edge of the window tidk searched, so it may continue beyond it

    The contig summary reads the rows of this table by their status, and only present rows are telomeres:

    status In the contig summary
    present a telomere: the copies in telomere_start and telomere_end are telomere_length divided by the unit length, rounded, and the two offset columns are tidk's offset. An array more than 1,000 bp inside the contig is also named in the reason column, for example (the start array is 2814 bp from the end); the verdict does not change
    wrong_strand not a telomere; the reason names it (array in the opposite orientation at the end)
    other_end, not_terminal nothing

    An array more than 1,000 bp inside is named because a telomere is the very end of a chromosome, so sequence beyond the array is a misjoin or an interstitial telomeric repeat.

    An array further inside than the window is not seen at all

    A wider telomere_window finds it, and the worked example shows an array that a narrow window would miss.

    The log line and the NOTE¶

    The log logs/telomere_units_{sample}.log holds one line that sums up the choice, followed by the NOTE when there is one, and the rule copies that line to the Snakemake console:

    [FunFlux] telomere_units <sample>: telomere repeat unit AACCCT (TTAGGG read on the other strand): 34 contig ends, 15 contigs with a telomere at both ends; known from the literature (filamentous Ascomycota, most Basidiomycota, Mucoromycota; Botrytis cinerea) and from 20 fungal species in the database
    Text Appears when
    the part in parentheses after the unit the published spelling differs from the canonical form; for a configured unit it says set by telomere_motif
    the same line, reading ... no telomere repeat identified (best candidate <UNIT>: <n> ends in the telomere orientation, <m> opposite) no candidate is eligible
    a second line, [FunFlux] NOTE telomere_units <sample>: ..., also copied to the console the chosen unit is unknown to both the literature table and the database, so it may be a telomere repeat nobody has reported or a satellite that happens to point one way; or a configured parameters.contig_summary.telomere_motif is not the data's first choice, because another unit scores higher or the configured one has no support

    A NOTE is a reason to open the candidates table, which shows the numbers behind either case.

    Genes: the join to the scaffold names¶

    genes is the one column that cannot be joined to the draft by name, because funannotate sort renames the contigs scaffold_1, scaffold_2, ... longest first (funannotate sort). The script therefore joins the GFF3 to the draft by sequence:

    Draft contig Matched to its scaffold by
    unchanged since the draft the identical sequence: a draft contig and a scaffold with the identical sequence are the same contig
    polished after the draft (Medaka for ONT samples, Polypolish in hybrid mode), so it differs from its scaffold by a base here and there five 300-bp probes taken from inside the contig, searched in the scaffolds whose length is within 5 % of its own

    The annotated_as column gives the result of this join. It links the two sets of contig names, which none of the other result files does.

    A worked example: Botrytis cinerea B05.10¶

    The public benchmark genome, Botrytis cinerea B05.10 (RefSeq assembly GCF_000143535.2), shows how the table reads on a real assembly. It has 18 chromosomes: 16 core chromosomes of 2.0 to 4.1 Mb and two minichromosomes of 247 and 209 kb. Its mitochondrial genome is 82 kb, and the reference assembly carries telomere repeats at 27 of its 36 chromosome ends.

    A longread run of public ONT reads of the strain (about 136x), assembled with hifiasm, gave 33 contigs: 32 in the nuclear genome and the mitochondrion. Validation on public genomes describes the runs. The telomere unit chosen from the data is AACCCT, that is TTAGGG, with arrays of 102 to 126 bp (17 to 21 copies) at 34 of the 66 contig ends. Every one of these arrays points the way a telomere points, with the C-rich strand at every contig start and the G-rich strand at every contig end, so there is no opposite end. Of the 33 contigs, 15 have an array at both ends. With the default settings and a median depth of 24.5, the table calls the 33 contigs:

    Verdict Contigs What they are
    chromosome 15 13 of the 16 core chromosomes, and the two minichromosomes
    chromosome_arm 3 the other 3 core chromosomes, each with one end without a telomere array
    rDNA_array 13 pieces of the rDNA array
    mitochondrion 1 the mitochondrial genome
    low_depth 1 an alternative copy of a region, or noise

    The 18 chromosomes of the reference come out as 18 contigs, 15 of them with telomere arrays at both ends. The other 15 contigs are rDNA pieces, the mitochondrion and one low-depth contig. A few rows, with the columns that decided:

    contig length depth_x_median telomere_start telomere_end rdna_units busco_markers genes annotated_as verdict
    ptg000005l 4,109,401 1.00 18 17 0 287 1,127 scaffold_1 chromosome
    ptg000007l 3,044,302 1.00 18 0 0 193 869 scaffold_4 chromosome_arm
    ptg000014l 2,512,653 0.99 18 0 6 184 701 scaffold_9 chromosome_arm
    ptg000022l 208,977 0.79 18 17 0 0 21 scaffold_18 chromosome
    ptg000004l 282,192 0.77 17 17 0 0 22 scaffold_17 chromosome
    ptg000030l 175,720 1.15 18 0 20 0 1 scaffold_19 rDNA_array
    ptg000011l 58,954 0.16 0 0 6 0 0 scaffold_25 rDNA_array
    ptg000015l 61,330 0.27 0 0 6 0 NA dropped by funannotate clean rDNA_array
    ptg000028c 82,351 7.37 0 0 0 0 NA not annotated (mitochondrion) mitochondrion
    ptg000029l 42,998 0.07 0 0 0 1 5 scaffold_27 low_depth

    What the rows show:

    • The core chromosomes. The 16 contigs of 2.0 to 4.1 Mb are the 16 core chromosomes. Of these, 13 are chromosome and 3 are chromosome_arm, each with one end without a telomere array. ptg000014l carries 6 rDNA units and 701 genes, so verdict rule 3 does not apply and it stays a chromosome arm. The start array of ptg000007l begins 2,814 bp inside the contig, and its reason says so: telomere repeats at the start only (18 copies) (the start array is 2814 bp from the end). Three chromosomes have one array more than 1,000 bp inside the contig end, named in their reasons the same way: 1,134 bp in ptg000002l, 3,660 bp in ptg000009l and 12,300 bp in ptg000017l.
    • The minichromosomes. The two contigs of 200 to 300 kb are the two minichromosomes, chromosome whatever their size. ptg000022l (209 kb) has its arrays right at the contig ends and 21 genes. ptg000004l (282 kb) has arrays of 17 copies that lie 19,746 and 15,234 bp inside the contig ends. The contig therefore carries about 35 kb beyond its telomeres, and the reference chromosome is 247 kb. Its reason names both offsets: telomere repeats at both ends (17 and 17 copies) (the start array is 19746 bp from the end; the end array is 15234 bp from the end); depth 0.77x the median; 22 genes, 0 BUSCO markers. With a window of 3,000 bp, neither array would be seen and the contig would read nuclear_contig, which is why the default window is 25 kb. The depths of the two minichromosomes, 0.79 and 0.77 times the median, are within the normal range.
    • The rDNA pieces. ptg000030l is a 176-kb rDNA array with a telomere at its start (18 copies), 20 rDNA units and one predicted gene, a gene call inside an rDNA unit. With fewer genes than rDNA units, it is an rDNA_array by verdict rule 3, with the telomere named in the reason. The 13 rDNA_array contigs are 36 to 176 kb of rDNA units at 0.16 to 1.15 times the median: the array is assembled in pieces and partly collapsed. Two of them, ptg000015l and ptg000024l, were dropped by funannotate clean as duplicates, so their genes cell is NA and annotated_as says so. The genus cell of the rDNA pieces names another genus than Botrytis, because a BLAST genus label is only as good as the closest sequence in the database (Decontamination). The rDNA unit holds lone copies of TTAGGG and CCCTAA, but a telomere call needs an array of at least 5 copies, so none of the other rDNA pieces gets a telomere.
    • The mitochondrion. ptg000028c is confirmed by the mitochondrion screen. It is circular, at 7.4 times the median depth, and not annotated.
    • The low-depth contig. ptg000029l, 43 kb at 0.07 times the median with 5 genes, is low_depth, an alternative copy of a region or noise.

    The candidates table shows how the unit was chosen. telomere_candidates.tsv has 20 rows, and these are the three units with the most copies at the contig ends:

    Unit Copies at the contig ends Ends in the telomere orientation Opposite ends Score
    AACCCT 657 34 0 34
    ACCTCC 164 0 3 -3
    AGATGG 152 0 4 -4

    Only the telomere unit has arrays that point the telomere way. The two microsatellites that follow it have three and four arrays at contig ends, all pointing the wrong way for a telomere. AACCCT has 657 copies at the contig ends and 661 in the whole assembly, so nearly every copy sits at an end, while AGATGG has 405 in the whole assembly. Seven units have a single end in the telomere orientation and are not eligible. The table marks AACCCT known from the literature and from 20 fungal species in the database. The log of telomere_units ends with the summary line quoted above and has no NOTE.

    A short-read assembly of the same strain chooses the same unit from thinner evidence. It was made with SPAdes from public Illumina reads and has 413 contigs, 4 of them mitochondrial:

    Unit Copies at the contig ends Ends in the telomere orientation Opposite ends Score Outcome
    AACCCT 227 13 1 12 chosen; no contig with an array at both ends
    AAGAG 33 13 5 8 eligible, ranks second
    AGAGGG 115 12 6 6 not eligible: 12 is not more than twice 6
    AAAGAG 179 5 5 0 arrays point either way
    AAGGAG 171 6 7 -1 arrays point either way

    Here the copy number cannot choose the unit, but the orientation still can. The microsatellites AAAGAG and AAGGAG have nearly as many copies at the contig ends as AACCCT, but their arrays point either way, so they score 0 and -1. tidk ends first reports 7 opposite ends for AACCCT. Six of them are the same array seen from the other end of a short contig of 1.4 to 12 kb, which the same-array rule relabels other_end. The seventh is a 30-bp run 12.4 kb inside a long contig.

    The gene count is FunFlux's own prediction. Where a published annotation exists, as here, its gene count is an independent check of the run as a whole. The table itself reads no reference.

    Settings¶

    The contig summary has five settings in parameters.contig_summary, and the address of the telomere database in links:

    parameters:
  contig_summary:
    telomere_motif: auto
    telomere_db_sha256: 1d4f5c8c2cce6a76aaa8de01602317a8e0b8d0401711b96f3c7a826c385da699
    telomere_window: 25000
    min_telomere_copies: 5
    min_rdna_units: 3
links:
  telomere_db_link: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/tolkit/a-telomeric-repeat-database/73c635c7b80e5ab369b4c7ec67d23db32dcaa1b8/data/telo_database_v2.tsv
    Key Default Allowed values What it does
    telomere_motif auto auto (any case), or letters A, C, G and T, 4 to 30 long; lower case is upper-cased auto: the unit is chosen from the data. A unit: used as given, the data's choice reported next to it (Setting the unit).
    telomere_db_sha256 the checksum of the database file at the template address 64 hexadecimal characters The SHA-256 the downloaded database must have. Change it together with links.telomere_db_link.
    telomere_window 25000 an integer from 500 to 100000 The terminal window: the bases at each contig end in which the candidate units are collected, and how far from the end tidk ends accepts an array. The array can sit some kb inside a contig end when the assembler ran past the telomere, so the window must cover that; the offset columns show where each array lies.
    min_telomere_copies 5 an integer ≥ 2 Copies of the unit an array needs: tidk ends requires this many unit lengths (30 bp for a 6-bp unit), and tidk explore lists a unit only when it forms a tandem run of this many copies.
    min_rdna_units 3 an integer ≥ 1 rDNA units (ITS windows) from which a contig can be an rDNA_array.

    The five values are checked before any job starts, like the other blocks (Configuration). A value outside the range stops the run with a [FunFlux] parameters.contig_summary.<key>: ... message. Every dry run prints the settings in one line: Contig summary: telomere motif auto (the repeat unit is chosen from the data), a telomere from 5 tandem copies within 25000 bp of a contig end, an rDNA array from 3 rDNA unit(s). A changed value re-runs the steps that use it (Re-runs).

    In the MultiQC report¶

    For the report, 02.assembly/{sample}/eval/contig_summary_mqc.tsv holds the same table prepared for MultiQC, with the rows named {sample}: {contig} and a description that names the telomere unit of the sample. MultiQC shows the tables of all samples of the run as one table in the section Contig summary of 06.report/multiqc_report.html. It writes the numbers to 06.report/multiqc_data/multiqc_contig_summary.txt. The table has all 20 columns, telomere_unit among them, so samples of different species keep their units apart. The column settings of the table show or hide columns.

    Above 500 rows, MultiQC shows a violin plot instead of the table

    MultiQC shows a table with more than 500 rows (its max_table_rows setting) as a violin plot instead, so for a batch of fragmented assemblies read contig_summary.tsv itself.

    Re-runs¶

    Each change in the left column re-runs the contig summary steps on the right:

    Change Re-runs
    a new draft telomere_units and contig_summary
    a new database file telomere_units
    parameters.contig_summary.telomere_motif, telomere_window or min_telomere_copies telomere_units and contig_summary
    links.telomere_db_link or telomere_db_sha256 telomere_db, then telomere_units of every sample and the steps after it
    a new telomere unit or ends table contig_summary
    min_rdna_units contig_summary
    a new screen result, a new busco.lineage (the BUSCO markers are counted per contig), new ITS windows or a new annotation contig_summary

    A re-run of these steps costs seconds to a minute and never touches the annotation. MultiQC follows each of them.

    Limits¶

    Some genomes, assemblies and settings leave a piece of evidence missing or unreliable. The table lists these situations and what the contig summary does in each:

    Situation What the table does
    telomeres that vary from copy to copy: Saccharomyces, Schizosaccharomyces, Cryptococcus only exact units are found, and no tandem run of one unit exists: tidk explore lists no unit, tidk ends finds no array, and the unit comes out as none even when the irregular repeat is written into parameters.contig_summary.telomere_motif
    telomere repeats that differ between chromosome ends: the Microstromatales yeasts no single unit covers more than a few of them
    a unit that no paper and no database names it is still found when it forms an array of at least min_telomere_copies copies within the window at the contig ends, because every tandem unit of the contig ends is a candidate; the NOTE then says that the unit is unknown
    a genuine telomere unit that fails one of the three filters it would be missed; none is known, and parameters.contig_summary.telomere_motif bypasses the filters
    an assembly with few chromosome ends, such as a fragmented short-read assembly few votes: a unit needs at least 2 telomere ends and more than twice as many telomere ends as opposite ends; when it falls short no unit is chosen, so set parameters.contig_summary.telomere_motif when the repeat is known
    an array further inside a contig than telomere_window it is not seen; the worked example shows the trade-off of a wider window
    an array of the chosen unit pointing the other way at a contig end (wrong_strand) not a telomere call; the reason names it, and the verdict rests on the rest
    a contig that ends at a centromere centromeres are not detected: it is a chromosome_arm like any other
    contigs mode, where depth is unknown (it is known only in the read-based modes) verdict rules 6 and 7 never apply; a mitochondrion that the screen did not confirm, or a contig at twice the depth of the genome, is judged on the other evidence alone: a gene-less contig at twice the depth is then a nuclear_fragment, not a repeat
    parameters.mitochondria.mode: off, read-based modes the screen confirms nothing, and the mitochondrion is judged on the rest of its evidence: its depth, several times the median, keeps it out of every verdict but repeat (when no gene was predicted on it) or unclassified
    parameters.mitochondria.mode: off, contigs mode without a depth, the mitochondrion is a nuclear_contig when genes were predicted on it
    parameters.mitochondria.mode: report the confirmed contig stays in the nuclear genome and is annotated; the table still calls it mitochondrion (verdict rule 1, from the screen's class), and its annotated_as cell names the scaffold_N under which funannotate annotated it, with its genes cell filled (Mitochondria)
    a fungus whose 5.8S matches none of ITSx's profiles rdna_units counts the 5.8S genes that nhmmer found with ITSx's profiles (Why the genome is cut into windows), so it is 0 on every contig, and the rDNA pieces are judged on depth and genes alone: low_depth, repeat, nuclear_fragment, nuclear_contig or unclassified
    the gene count it is the run's own gene prediction, counted per scaffold: evidence that a contig is nuclear sequence, not a measure of annotation quality
    a polished contig (Medaka in longread and hybrid mode, Polypolish in hybrid mode) its join to its scaffold_N rests on five 300-bp probes and a length within 5 %; dropped by funannotate clean on a contig that is in contigs_sort.fasta means the join failed, and the genes cell of that contig is then NA

    Where evidence is missing or unreliable, the verdict rests on the evidence that remains

    For the fungi of the first two rows, the table still gives the depth, rDNA, BUSCO and gene evidence, and the verdicts chromosome and chromosome_arm do not occur. The annotated_as column shows whether each contig found its scaffold.

    Output files¶

    Every file is kept, and contig_summary.tsv is the one to read:

    Path Content
    00.resources/telomeres/telo_database_v2.tsv the telomere repeat database of tidk, downloaded once per output folder
    00.resources/telomeres/PROVENANCE.txt source URL, download date, checksum and row count of the database
    02.assembly/{sample}/eval/telomere_candidates.tsv every candidate telomere unit with its counts (The three telomere files)
    02.assembly/{sample}/eval/telomere_unit.txt the telomere unit of the assembly, or none
    02.assembly/{sample}/eval/telomere_ends.tsv the arrays of that unit at every contig end
    02.assembly/{sample}/eval/contig_summary.tsv the table, one row per contig of the draft
    02.assembly/{sample}/eval/contig_summary_mqc.tsv the same table for MultiQC
    logs/telomere_db.log the database download
    logs/telomere_units_{sample}.log the count line of the script, the one-line summary and the NOTE when there is one; tidk's own messages only when tidk fails
    logs/contig_summary_{sample}.log the one-line summary

    Each step writes its files only when every input was read without error. The job stops on a missing file, a malformed database, a tidk failure, a table without an expected column, a depth that is not a number or a FASTA with two records of the same name. It reports the cause as one line in the log, [FunFlux] telomere_units: error: ... or contig_summary.py: error: ..., which for a tidk failure includes tidk's own message.

    The full output tree is on Output files.
