    Antifungal resistance mutations¶

    FunFlux can screen every genome for point mutations studied for resistance to antifungal drugs and fungicides. The AMR screen (the resistance-mutation screen) runs ChroQueTas against the FungAMR database (Bédard et al. 2025), which lists such mutations with the evidence for or against resistance. For every sample it writes 05.amr/{sample}/amr_summary.tsv, with one row per target protein of the species and the changes found. Each change gets a verdict from the evidence FungAMR holds: whether it confers resistance, to which drugs, and how strong the evidence is (Evidence levels and verdicts). The table also appears in the MultiQC report.

    ---
config:
  themeVariables:
    fontSize: 14px
  flowchart:
    nodeSpacing: 35
    rankSpacing: 35
---
flowchart TD
    TAB([species<br/>column 2 of the<br/>annotation parameter table]) --> MATCH[match to a species folder<br/>of the database]
    DB([ChroQueTas and its database<br/>downloaded once<br/>per output folder]) --> MATCH
    MATCH --> NUC[nuclear run<br/>contigs_final.fasta<br/>nuclear genetic code]
    MATCH -.->|species with cytochrome b| MIT[mitochondrial run<br/>mitochondrial genetic code]
    NUC --> CALL[each target read from the run<br/>of its own compartment<br/>verdicts from evidence levels]
    MIT --> CALL
    CALL --> OUT([05.amr/sample/<br/>amr_summary.tsv<br/>amr_positions.tsv])
    OUT --> REP([MultiQC report<br/>summary of every sample])

    The AMR screen is off by default. ChroQueTas and its database are licensed only for non-commercial use, and switching the screen on is your acceptance of that licence (Licence). To switch it on, set:

    parameters:
  amr:
    chroquetas: on

    Terms used on this page
    AMR screen antimicrobial resistance screen: the optional step, off by default, in which ChroQueTas looks in each genome for the changes FungAMR lists for the species
    FungAMR the database of mutations studied for resistance to antifungal drugs and fungicides, with evidence levels for and against; ChroQueTas screens each genome against part of it
    target protein a protein whose FungAMR positions ChroQueTas checks, such as Beta-tubulin or Cyp51; amr_summary.tsv has one row per target of the species
    FungAMR mutation the ChroQueTas call for a change that FungAMR lists at that position, whatever its evidence; it does not by itself mean resistance
    evidence level the FungAMR score for a change and a drug, by kind of study: 1 (strongest) to 8 for resistance found, -1 to -8 for none found
    verdict the reading of FungAMR's evidence for a change or a target: resistance, conflicting, no resistance, unknown, wild type, or not compared for a position
    compartment nuclear or mitochondrial: the ChroQueTas run, each with its own genetic code, from which a target protein's result is read
    contamination and mitochondrion screen the screen of the draft assembly: BLAST against core_nt, BlobTools, the mitochondrion screen and the contig selector; in contigs mode only with parameters.contigs.screen: true

    What the screen looks for¶

    Fungi become resistant through changes in their own genes. FungAMR records amino acid substitutions and other changes, such as more copies of a gene, and ChroQueTas screens the substitutions. Many of them sit in the protein the drug acts on, at a few known positions. The gene itself is present in every strain of the species, sensitive or resistant, so a search for resistance genes, as used for bacteria, does not fit. What tells a resistant strain from a sensitive one is the amino acid at those positions. The AMR screen therefore finds each target protein in the assembly and reads those positions.

    The table lists the main targets in the database, with drugs named in their entries.

    Target (database name) Protein Drugs, for example Species in the database with this target
    Cyp51 sterol 14α-demethylase of the ergosterol pathway azoles: fluconazole, voriconazole, itraconazole, posaconazole 27
    Cytochrome_b cytochrome b of respiratory complex III, encoded in the mitochondrial genome QoI fungicides (strobilurins): azoxystrobin, pyraclostrobin, trifloxystrobin 15
    Beta-tubulin β-tubulin of the microtubules benzimidazoles: carbendazim, benomyl, thiophanate-methyl; diethofencarb 12
    Fks β-1,3-glucan synthase of the cell wall echinocandins: caspofungin, anidulafungin, micafungin 10
    Sdh succinate dehydrogenase, respiratory complex II SDHI fungicides: boscalid, carboxin, pydiflumetofen 4
    Bos1, OS1 one histidine kinase of the osmotic stress pathway, listed under two names with the same reference protein (UniProt Q8X1E7) dicarboximides (iprodione, procymidone), fludioxonil 1 (Botrytis cinerea)

    The database has 210 target proteins in all, under 109 target names. The others include efflux pumps and their regulators (Cdr1, Pdr1, Tac1, Mrr1) and the enzymes that turn 5-fluorocytosine into its toxic form (Fcy1, Fur1).

    In two species the database holds one protein under two names: Botrytis cinerea Bos1 and OS1, and Fusarium graminearum Myosin-1 and Myosin-5. Each name gets its own row in the tables, so a change at a position both names list is reported twice.

    FungAMR and ChroQueTas¶

    FungAMR (Bédard et al. 2025) was curated by hand from 501 published studies. It holds 35,792 entries for 208 drugs, 246 genes and 95 fungal species. Each entry gives the gene, the mutation and the drug, with a confidence score for the evidence (Evidence levels and verdicts). FungAMR can be searched online in the Comprehensive Antibiotic Resistance Database (CARD).

    ChroQueTas, from the same paper, screens a genome against the part of FungAMR prepared for it. FunFlux runs version 1.0.1, downloaded from one fixed commit of its GitHub repository, as described under Settings. ChroQueTas takes each target protein of the species through four steps:

    Step Tool What happens
    1 miniprot 0.14 aligns the reference protein to the genome, across introns, and translates the gene it finds with the genetic code of the run
    2 BLASTP of BLAST+ 2.16.0 compares the extracted protein with the reference, and ChroQueTas keeps it at more than 90 % identity over more than 75 % of the reference
    3 MAFFT 7.525 aligns the extracted protein with the reference
    4 ChroQueTas reads the amino acid at every FungAMR position of the target and calls it

    Every position gets one of the calls below. FunFlux writes ChroQueTas's FungAMR MUTATION as FungAMR mutation, its New mutation as new mutation, and so on.

    Call Meaning
    FungAMR mutation the amino acid differs from the reference, and FungAMR lists this change at this position, whatever the evidence says: for resistance, against it, or both. ChroQueTas adds the drugs with their evidence levels, and the verdict says which way they point (Evidence levels and verdicts)
    new mutation the amino acid differs from the reference, but FungAMR does not list this change at this position. It is a possible new resistance mutation that needs its own investigation (ChroQueTas README)
    no mutation the reference amino acid
    no alignment the found protein has a gap at the position. The gap is a deletion, a part of the gene missing from the assembly (as at a contig end), or a premature stop codon, which MAFFT turns into a gap. A nonsense mutation that FungAMR lists (written X) therefore shows up here, not as a FungAMR mutation. The copy's protein, <name>_<target>.<fragment>.faa, tells which, because a stop is written * there
    position not found the position lies outside the alignment, or it is not a number, or its codon could not be read (found amino acid X)

    The last two calls are not comparisons. They do not count as positions checked, and the summary's note names them. A target with no compared position at all is not evaluated (Statuses).

    Three database rows have no position number. ChroQueTas reports the insertion (Cryptococcus deuterogattii Uxs1, 1182ins(fs)) as position not found, and skips the duplication (Nakaseomyces glabratus Pdr1, c.3308dupT) without a word. It reads the translocation (Candidozyma auris Ncp1) as position 1, and FunFlux reports that row as position not found too.

    ChroQueTas calls a position a FungAMR mutation when the letter it found is one of those the database lists there, and a new mutation when it is a letter other than the reference's. In three cases the letter is not a change, and FunFlux writes another call:

    What ChroQueTas found ChroQueTas's call FunFlux's call
    X: miniprot's letter for a codon it cannot read, one with N bases (a gap in a scaffold). FungAMR writes a stop as X too, but miniprot writes a stop as * FungAMR MUTATION where FungAMR lists a stop, New mutation elsewhere position not found
    a gap where FungAMR lists a deletion (written -, 22 rows), beyond the part of the reference the found protein covers (BLASTP's sstart to send): the protein ends early, as for a gene cut at a contig end FungAMR MUTATION no alignment
    the reference amino acid, at the one row that lists the reference amino acid as the change: Saccharomyces cerevisiae Hap1 N832N (clotrimazole, level 4) FungAMR MUTATION no mutation

    A gap inside the found protein, where FungAMR lists a deletion, stays a FungAMR mutation. As on every row, fungamr_drugs keeps ChroQueTas's drug text.

    ChroQueTas does not screen the whole of FungAMR. The database of version 1.0.1 holds only single mutations, and only species with a reliable reference protein (ChroQueTas README):

    FungAMR ChroQueTas database 1.0.1
    entries 35,792 8,285 mutations: only single mutations, tested on their own rather than in combination with others
    species 95 57: only species for which an accurate reference protein exists; a species without one is left out, because a poor reference could give spurious calls

    An entry can carry evidence for resistance, against it, or both (Evidence levels and verdicts).

    Only single mutations in the target proteins are screened

    The AMR screen does not detect gene duplication or loss, gene disruption, translocation, aneuploidy, combinations of mutations, or any change outside the protein, such as an insertion in a promoter that raises the expression of the target.

    Evidence levels and verdicts¶

    The verdicts rest on the evidence levels of FungAMR. FungAMR gives each drug of a change two confidence scores, which ChroQueTas writes as Drug(positive/negative) (Bédard et al. 2025; ChroQueTas wiki, "Confidence score for antimicrobial resistance"):

    Score Meaning
    positive, 1 to 8 evidence that the change causes resistance to that drug; 1 is the strongest, 8 the weakest
    negative, -1 to -8 evidence, from the same kinds of study, that it does not: the change was reported in susceptible strains; -1 is the strongest
    NA no report of that kind

    When several studies report a change, each score is the strongest of its kind.

    The level names the kind of study. FunFlux writes it in words after the number, and the same words serve both signs. After a positive level the study found resistance, and after a negative one it found none.

    Level What the positive level means (ChroQueTas wiki) Words in the tables
    1 the change was made in a susceptible strain of the same species, and its effect on resistance was measured the change made in the species and measured
    2 the change was made in the same gene expressed in another species, possibly at another level of expression, and its effect measured the change made in the gene, expressed in another species
    3 its effect was measured in a bulk competition assay, such as deep mutational scanning bulk competition assay
    4 it arose in experimental evolution, often enough in independent replicates to suggest that it causes resistance experimental evolution
    5 deleting the gene causes resistance gene deletion
    6 overexpressing or duplicating the gene causes resistance gene overexpression or duplication
    7 a significant association with resistance in a population: GWAS, QTL mapping or classical genetics population association study
    8 found in a resistant natural strain, such as a clinical isolate, without further validation natural isolates, not validated further

    A negative level is the same kind of study with the opposite result. -8, for example, records a natural isolate with the change that was not resistant, and -3 a deep mutational scan in which the change gave no resistance.

    A FungAMR mutation is not always a resistance mutation¶

    ChroQueTas calls every change that FungAMR lists a FungAMR mutation, whatever its scores, and more than half of the changes in its database carry evidence against resistance only. In ChroQueTas 1.0.1 the evidence splits as follows:

    Evidence Negative only Positive only Both No drug All
    rows of the database (changes), all drugs of the row together 4,655 2,943 686 1 8,285
    drugs of those rows, one by one 19,397 8,868 108 28,373

    Most of the negative-only rows, 4,596, belong to Candida albicans Cyp51 and Saccharomyces cerevisiae Fcy1, and all but five of those come from deep mutational scans (level -3). FunFlux therefore sorts the drugs of every FungAMR mutation by their evidence. Candida albicans Cyp51 A61V, for example, reads Clotrimazole(3/NA),Fluconazole(NA/-2),Isavuconazole(3/NA),Itraconazole(3/-2),Posaconazole(2/NA),Voriconazole(3/-2), and its drugs are sorted like this:

    A drug with Goes to In A61V
    a positive level only resistance_to clotrimazole, isavuconazole, posaconazole
    a negative level only no_resistance_to fluconazole
    both both columns, each with its own level: a conflicting drug itraconazole, voriconazole

    Each position then gets a verdict. For a FungAMR mutation, it is the first of its four rows below that applies:

    Call Verdict When
    FungAMR mutation resistance at least one drug has a positive level only
    FungAMR mutation conflicting no drug has a positive level only, and at least one has both
    FungAMR mutation no resistance every drug named has a negative level only
    FungAMR mutation unknown no drug is named
    new mutation unknown a change FungAMR does not list, at a position where it lists others
    no mutation wild type the reference amino acid
    no alignment, position not found not compared the position could not be compared

    A61V is resistance, although two of its drugs conflict. A change is conflicting only when no drug has resistance evidence alone, as for Aspergillus niger Cyp51 Q228R, Voriconazole(8/-8). FunFlux does not weigh one kind of evidence against the other, and both levels stay in the tables. The 8,285 rows of the database get these verdicts:

    Verdict Rows
    resistance 3,610, of which 42 have a conflicting drug as well
    no resistance 4,655
    conflicting 19
    unknown 1: the Cryptococcus deuterogattii Uxs1 insertion, which names no drug; ChroQueTas does not compare it (FungAMR and ChroQueTas)

    A target takes the first of these verdicts found among its compared positions, all copies together: resistance, conflicting, unknown, no resistance, wild type. A change of unknown effect ranks above no resistance and wild type, because it needs a look. A target that is not evaluated or not found, and a sample that is not screened, get -, and the status says why.

    Botrytis cinerea β-tubulin E198V, for example, reads Benzimidazole(8/NA),Carbendazim(7/NA),Diethofencarb(8/NA),Iprodione(NA/-8) in the database. A genome with valine at position 198 gets these cells in amr_positions.tsv:

    Column Cell
    verdict resistance
    resistance_to Benzimidazole (8: natural isolates, not validated further); Carbendazim (7: population association study); Diethofencarb (8: natural isolates, not validated further)
    no_resistance_to Iprodione (-8: natural isolates, not validated further)

    That is evidence that E198V causes resistance to three drugs, and reports of natural isolates with E198V that were not resistant to iprodione.

    What a level does not say¶

    A positive level says how good the evidence is that a change causes resistance, and a negative level how good the evidence is that it does not. Neither is a measurement of the isolate. Whether this strain is resistant, and how strongly, takes a susceptibility test. The FungAMR authors put it the same way: a confidence score is not a probability of treatment failure, but reflects the evidence that a change confers resistance under laboratory conditions (Bédard et al. 2025).

    The verdict no resistance says only what the reports found

    Evidence of no resistance is the harder kind to judge. A susceptible result can come from an assay that was not sensitive enough, and such assays rarely test a resistant control alongside. The FungAMR authors kept these reports in the database but left them out of their own analyses for that reason (Bédard et al. 2025). The verdict no resistance therefore means that the reports found no resistance to the drugs named. It does not show that the change never matters for resistance, and it says nothing about drugs that were not tested.

    Levels 7 and 8 rest on association, not on a test of the change itself. The change was found in resistant isolates, or linked to resistance in a population. Field isolates are often resistant to several classes of fungicides at once. A drug at level 7 or 8 that acts on another target can therefore record a resistance found together with the change rather than caused by it. In Botrytis cinerea, for example, iprodione (level 8) is listed for β-tubulin E198A, and carbendazim (level 7) and boscalid (level 8) for cytochrome b G143A. Such a drug counts towards the verdict resistance like any other.

    The species¶

    Each sample is screened against the database folder of its species. The species is column 2 of the annotation parameter table (files.annotation_params), the name funannotate uses as well. The database names its species folders Genus_species. FunFlux matches the name to a folder, ignoring case, and takes the first of three tries that succeeds:

    1. The whole name, with spaces turned into underscores and f. sp. (or f.sp.) removed: Blumeria graminis f. sp. hordei gives Blumeria_graminis_hordei.
    2. The first two words: Zymoseptoria tritici IPO323 gives Zymoseptoria_tritici.

    3. A short list of other names, older or newer, of species whose database folder carries a different name. Each pair is one taxon in NCBI Taxonomy:

      Name in the annotation table Database folder
      Candida glabrata Nakaseomyces_glabratus
      Candida auris Candidozyma_auris
      Parastagonospora nodorum Phaeosphaeria_nodorum
      Pseudocercospora fijiensis Mycosphaerella_fijiensis
      Fulvia fulva, Cladosporium fulvum Passalora_fulva
      Oculimacula yallundae Tapesia_yallundae
      Oculimacula acuformis Tapesia_acuformis
      Issatchenkia orientalis, Candida krusei Pichia_kudriavzevii
      Candida lusitaniae Clavispora_lusitaniae
      Lodderomyces metapsilosis Candida_metapsilosis
      Lodderomyces orthopsilosis Candida_orthopsilosis
      Pyricularia grisea Magnaporthe_grisea
      Blumeria hordei Blumeria_graminis_hordei

    Only renamings of one species are listed. Pyricularia oryzae, the rice blast fungus, is not matched to Magnaporthe_grisea, because NCBI Taxonomy keeps them as two species (Magnaporthe grisea is Pyricularia grisea).

    A name that matches no folder leaves the sample not screened, and the run goes on. amr_summary.tsv gets one row with status not screened, and a NOTE in the log and on the console names the database folders of the same genus, if there are any. 00.resources/chroquetas/species.txt lists the 57 folders.

    A species outside the database is not screened against a relative

    FunFlux never screens a sample against another species of its genus, because the positions of a resistance mutation are positions in the reference protein of one species. For a species outside the database, FungAMR itself may still list mutations of its targets. Search it in CARD (https://card.mcmaster.ca/fungamrhome, the address ChroQueTas gives) and compare the positions by hand with the predicted proteins in 04.annotation/{sample}/funannotate/annotate_results/.

    Two runs: the nuclear and the mitochondrial genome¶

    ChroQueTas translates the genome with one genetic code per run. The nuclear and the mitochondrial genes of one fungus use different codes, so one run cannot translate both correctly. These are the codes that matter here (NCBI, The Genetic Codes):

    NCBI table Used by Differs from the standard code in
    1, the standard code the nuclear genes of most fungi
    12 the nuclear genes of the yeasts of the CTG clade; ChroQueTas chooses 12 for its 8 species of that clade and 1 for the others CTG read as serine
    4, the mold mitochondrial code fungal mitochondria TGA codes for tryptophan instead of a stop
    3, the yeast mitochondrial code the mitochondria of Saccharomyces cerevisiae and Candida glabrata TGA read as tryptophan too, ATA as methionine and CTN as threonine

    Cytochrome b is the one target of the database encoded in the mitochondrial genome, and it is held for 15 species. Translated with the nuclear code, its gene gives a poorer protein, and the example below shows how much. Rule amr_chroquetas therefore runs ChroQueTas twice, each run in its own folder:

    Run Input Genetic code Results read from it
    nuclear, chroquetas/nuclear/ 02.assembly/{sample}/contigs_final.fasta chosen by ChroQueTas for the species every target except Cytochrome_b
    mitochondrial, chroquetas/mitochondrial/ contigs_mitochondrial.fasta when the contamination and mitochondrion screen splits the mitochondrion off (parameters.mitochondria.mode: separate_confirmed, the default); otherwise contigs_final.fasta parameters.amr.mito_genetic_code; auto: 3 for the four Saccharomycetaceae of the database (Saccharomyces cerevisiae, S. paradoxus, Nakaseomyces glabratus, Kluyveromyces marxianus), 4 for every other species, as NCBI Taxonomy records them Cytochrome_b

    A target is read only from the run of its own compartment, and a target the other run also extracts, translated with the wrong code, is ignored. The mitochondrial run reads the confirmed mitochondrion when the contamination and mitochondrion screen splits it off, and the whole assembly when nothing is split off. Whether the mitochondrial run happens, and what it reads, in each case:

    Case Mitochondrial run Cytochrome_b
    the species' database folder does not hold Cytochrome_b none not a target of the species
    the contamination and mitochondrion screen splits the mitochondrion off and confirmed a contig on contigs_mitochondrial.fasta read from this run
    the contamination and mitochondrion screen splits the mitochondrion off but confirmed no contig none: contigs_mitochondrial.fasta is empty not evaluated (no mitochondrial contig)
    contigs mode without the contamination and mitochondrion screen, or any mode with parameters.mitochondria.mode off or report: nothing is split off on contigs_final.fasta, among whose contigs the mitochondrion lies if the assembly holds it read from this run

    Statuses¶

    The status tells whether the positions of a target could be compared. Every target of the species gets one in amr_summary.tsv:

    Status Meaning
    evaluated at least one copy of the protein passed step 2 and had at least one FungAMR position compared; amr_positions.tsv lists them
    not evaluated no position was compared: the protein was found but has insertions against the reference; or it was found, but every FungAMR position lies in a gap of the found protein, at a codon that could not be read, or outside the protein (no alignment, position not found); or ChroQueTas skipped it; or, for Cytochrome_b, there was no mitochondrial contig to search. The note says which
    not found no copy passed step 2: miniprot found no alignment of the reference protein in the genome (ChroQueTas's log says We could not extract <target> from the genome), or what it found does not pass the filter of step 2 (more than 90 % identity over more than 75 % of the reference). The gene is absent from the assembly, or too different from the reference
    not screened the species is not in the database (The species); one row for the sample, target -

    For a copy whose positions it does not compare, ChroQueTas writes WARNING: genome contains insertions for <target> and thus AMR are not calculated instead of the positions. The line has two causes, which FunFlux reports differently:

    • Insertions. The extracted protein has residues the reference lacks. FunFlux counts the copy as found, with its positions not compared: not evaluated.
    • A copy below the thresholds of step 2. ChroQueTas then keeps no .blastp.txt file for it. FunFlux counts the copy as not found, and a target with no other copy is not found.

    A common cause of insertions is an intron the protein aligner could not place, such as an intron of a mitochondrial cytochrome b gene (the example below).

    ChroQueTas 1.0.1 also skips a target silently in one case. When a target is not found, it removes the name from its list of targets by a text replacement that also cuts that name out of longer names. Vma16 is therefore lost when Vma1 was not found. Such a target is not evaluated, with a note that says so.

    ChroQueTas numbers the copies of a target it finds as fragment 1, 2, and so on. A second copy can be a gene duplication, a gene split across two contigs of a fragmented assembly, or aneuploidy (ChroQueTas README). Each copy is compared on its own, and the summary marks the changes of a later copy, for example E198A (copy 2) (resistance).

    The tables¶

    amr_summary.tsv and amr_positions.tsv share a few conventions. In both tables - marks an empty cell. Drug names are FungAMR's, with underscores for spaces. A few contain a comma, such as Fludioxonil,_Iprodione (one entry, both drugs together), so the drugs of a FunFlux cell are separated by ;. Changes are written as reference amino acid, position and found amino acid, as in E198A.

    05.amr/{sample}/amr_summary.tsv has one row per target of the species:

    Column Content
    sample the sample name
    species the database folder the species was matched to, for example Botrytis_cinerea; for a sample that is not screened, column 2 of the annotation table as written there
    target the target protein as the database names it, for example Beta-tubulin; - for a sample that is not screened
    compartment nuclear or mitochondrial: the run the result comes from; - for a sample that is not screened
    status evaluated, not evaluated, not found or not screened (Statuses)
    positions_checked the FungAMR positions compared, over all compared copies; 0 when none
    verdict the first of resistance, conflicting, unknown, no resistance, wild type among the verdicts of the target's compared positions, all copies together (Verdicts); - for a target that is not evaluated or not found, and for a sample that is not screened
    fungamr_mutations the FungAMR mutations, each with its verdict, separated by ,: E198A (resistance); E198A (copy 2) (resistance) for a change in a second copy
    new_mutations the other changes at FungAMR positions, separated by ,: F200L, F200L (copy 2). Their verdict is unknown
    resistance_to the drugs with a positive level, from all FungAMR mutations of the target, each with the change and the level: Carbendazim (E198A, 7); Iprodione (E198A, 8). A drug named by two changes appears once for each; a change found on two copies names its drugs once
    no_resistance_to the drugs with a negative level, written the same way: Iprodione (E198V, -8)
    note for an evaluated target, the copies and positions that were not compared, and a target found more than once; otherwise why the target was not evaluated or not found, or why the sample was not screened

    05.amr/{sample}/amr_positions.tsv has one row per FungAMR position of every copy that ChroQueTas read position by position, including the positions it could not compare (a copy with insertions has no rows):

    Column Content
    sample, target, compartment as in the summary
    fragment the copy of the target: 1 for the first
    position the position in the reference protein
    reference_aa, found_aa the amino acid of the reference and of this genome
    call FungAMR mutation, new mutation, no mutation, no alignment or position not found
    verdict for a FungAMR mutation resistance, conflicting, no resistance or unknown; unknown for a new mutation; wild type for no mutation; not compared for the last two calls (Verdicts)
    resistance_to for a FungAMR mutation, the drugs with a positive level, each with the level and its words: Carbendazim (7: population association study); Iprodione (8: natural isolates, not validated further)
    no_resistance_to for a FungAMR mutation, the drugs with a negative level, the same way: Iprodione (-8: natural isolates, not validated further)
    known_resistance_changes for a compared position, the changes the database lists there with a positive level for at least one drug, in the order of the database file: E198A, E198G, E198K, E198V. On a wild type row, the changes that would matter there; on an unknown row, the changes known at that position
    fungamr_drugs ChroQueTas's text, unchanged: Carbendazim(7/NA),Iprodione(8/NA); - where it names no drug (NA, or Unknown for a new mutation). Its drugs are separated by commas, but a few names hold a comma (Fludioxonil,_Iprodione) or brackets (Azole(s)): split it at each comma that follows a closing bracket (),), not at every comma

    05.amr/{sample}/amr_summary_mqc.tsv is the same summary with header lines for MultiQC. They hold the section name and a description that names FungAMR, says what the verdicts mean and how to read the evidence levels. The rows of the file are named {sample}: {target}, so the tables of all samples merge into one table in the section AMR mutations (ChroQueTas) of 06.report/multiqc_report.html (Report).

    The log logs/amr_chroquetas_{sample}.log holds ChroQueTas's messages of both runs. It ends with one line, [FunFlux] amr_chroquetas <sample>: ..., which the rule copies to the Snakemake console. The line names the database folder used, the targets evaluated, not evaluated and not found, and the targets per verdict, as in verdicts: 1 resistance (Beta-tubulin), 2 wild type (Bos1, OS1). It then gives each FungAMR mutation found, with its verdict and the levels of its drugs, as in Beta-tubulin E198A (resistance: Carbendazim 7, Iprodione 8). A change with evidence against resistance only reads (no resistance: Iprodione -8). The NOTE of a sample that is not screened reaches the console the same way.

    ChroQueTas's own files¶

    05.amr/{sample}/chroquetas/nuclear/ and mitochondrial/ hold ChroQueTas's output as it wrote it, the files to read when a call needs checking. FunFlux hands the genome to ChroQueTas as genome.fasta in the nuclear run and as mito.fasta in the mitochondrial run, and ChroQueTas names its files after it (<name> below):

    File Content
    <name>.ChroQueTaS.AMR_summary.txt every FungAMR and new mutation of the run: Protein, Fragment, Position_reference, AA_reference, AA_query, Fungicide_resistance; or the single line No AMR mutations found in <name>
    <name>.ChroQueTaS.AMR_stats.txt per target, the number of FungAMR and new mutations; absent when there is none
    <name>.ChroQueTaS.<target>.<fragment>.tsv every FungAMR position of that copy: Position, Reference, Query, Result, Fungicides; or the single WARNING line of a protein whose positions were not compared
    <name>_<target>.<fragment>.faa the protein extracted from the genome
    <name>_<target>.<fragment>.paf miniprot's alignment of the reference protein to the genome; fields 6 to 9 give the contig and the coordinates
    <name>_<target>.<fragment>.blastp.txt the BLASTP comparison with the reference: identity, coverage, lengths, gaps; only for a protein that passed the thresholds
    tmp/ ChroQueTas's working files

    A target miniprot could not extract has no file at all, and a run that was not made has no folder. mitochondrial/ exists only when the species has cytochrome b and its mitochondrial run was made (Two runs).

    Files added to chroquetas/ are lost when amr_chroquetas runs again

    Snakemake deletes the whole chroquetas/ folder before amr_chroquetas runs again, so each run starts clean, and files you add there are lost.

    How the step runs¶

    Two rules make the tables. Both run only with parameters.amr.chroquetas: on.

    Rule Runs Does Writes
    chroquetas_db once per output folder downloads bin/ChroQueTas.sh, FungAMR_db/db-v20250811.tgz and LICENSE from links.chroquetas_link, checks the script and the archive against their SHA-256, unpacks the archive (Reference databases); conda environment download for curl and tar, and sha256sum of the machine 00.resources/chroquetas/
    amr_chroquetas once per sample, as soon as contigs_final.fasta exists (and contigs_mitochondrial.fasta where it is read) a FunFlux script matches the species to a database folder, runs ChroQueTas once or twice (Two runs) and writes the three tables; conda environment chroquetas; up to 4 threads; seconds 05.amr/{sample}/

    The FunFlux script runs with the Python 3.12 of the chroquetas environment. That environment holds the tools ChroQueTas calls, at the versions its authors pin in their own conda package: BLAST+ 2.16.0, EMBOSS 6.6.0, MAFFT 7.525, miniprot 0.14 and SeqKit 2.1.0. ChroQueTas itself is a bash script and is not in the environment. The FunFlux script runs it as bash ChroQueTas.sh ... from 00.resources/chroquetas/. ChroQueTas takes bash, GNU grep (it uses grep -P), sed and basic commands such as cut, paste and tr from the machine (Commands outside the conda environments).

    A failed ChroQueTas run stops the job with [FunFlux] amr_chroquetas: error: ... and the last 20 lines of ChroQueTas's log (Troubleshooting). A run in which no target could be extracted is not a failure, and every target is then not found.

    The AMR screen reads only the assembly, so it runs early, beside the annotation, without waiting for it.

    Licence, and why the step is off¶

    ChroQueTas and the FungAMR database that comes with it are licensed CC BY-NC-ND 4.0: use for non-commercial purposes only, with credit to the authors, and no distribution of modified copies. ChroQueTas is not packaged on bioconda or conda-forge, and its authors publish it on their own conda channel. FunFlux therefore handles both as follows.

    • It ships neither the script nor the database, and never changes them.
    • It downloads both at run time, unchanged, from one pinned commit of the authors' GitHub repository, and checks them against SHA-256 checksums before anything runs them.
    • It runs the script with the tools it needs from a pinned environment of bioconda and conda-forge packages.
    • It keeps the step off by default.

    Switching the AMR screen on accepts a non-commercial licence

    Setting parameters.amr.chroquetas: on is your acceptance of that licence, as naming a SignalP 6 archive is for DTU's. For commercial use, ask the authors.

    Every dry run prints whether the AMR screen is on: AMR screen: ChroQueTas on (licence CC BY-NC-ND 4.0: non-commercial use). or AMR screen: off. 00.resources/chroquetas/ holds the script, the database and their licence text. The licence allows passing them on unchanged, for non-commercial purposes and with credit (Licensing).

    A worked example: Botrytis cinerea B05.10¶

    The genome of the validation runs, the public Botrytis cinerea B05.10, was run in hybrid mode. Public ONT reads of the strain were assembled with hifiasm, and Polypolish corrected the assembly with the strain's public Illumina pairs. contigs_final.fasta holds 34 contigs, and contigs_mitochondrial.fasta the one mitochondrial contig. Column 2 of the annotation table reads Botrytis cinerea, which matches the folder Botrytis_cinerea at the first try. The folder holds three proteins under four target names. Beta-tubulin, and Bos1 and OS1, two entries of one histidine kinase, are in the nucleus, and Cytochrome_b is in the mitochondrion.

    The nuclear run, with the standard code ChroQueTas chose, took 4.2 s and about 0.5 GB of memory. The mitochondrial run, with code 4 (auto for this species), took 0.9 s. amr_summary.tsv reads, in part:

    target compartment status positions_checked verdict fungamr_mutations resistance_to no_resistance_to
    Beta-tubulin nuclear evaluated 3 resistance E198A (resistance) Carbendazim (E198A, 7); Iprodione (E198A, 8) -
    Bos1 nuclear evaluated 11 wild type - - -
    Cytochrome_b mitochondrial not evaluated 0 - - - -
    OS1 nuclear evaluated 2 wild type - - -

    The β-tubulin rows of amr_positions.tsv read, in part:

    position reference_aa found_aa call verdict resistance_to known_resistance_changes
    198 E A FungAMR mutation resistance Carbendazim (7: population association study); Iprodione (8: natural isolates, not validated further) E198A, E198G, E198K, E198V
    200 F F no mutation wild type - F200Y
    351 T T no mutation wild type - T351I

    B05.10 carries β-tubulin E198A, verdict resistance. Its histidine kinase (Bos1, OS1) is wild type, and cytochrome b is not evaluated, the expected outcome for this strain, as explained below.

    • β-tubulin E198A. The extracted protein matches the FungAMR reference (UniProt P53373) at 99.8 % identity over its whole length, and position 198 is alanine where the reference has glutamate. FungAMR holds evidence that E198A causes resistance to carbendazim at level 7 (a population-level association) and to iprodione at level 8, and none against it, so the verdict is resistance. The protein of the B05.10 reference annotation, BctubA (RefSeq XP_024546500.1), also has alanine at 198, so the call is the genotype of the strain, not an error of the assembly. The iprodione entry is of the kind described under What a level does not say: iprodione acts on the osmotic stress pathway, not on tubulin.
    • The known resistance changes. E198A is one of four changes at position 198 with evidence of resistance. Positions 200 and 351 carry the wild type, and their rows name the change that would matter there: F200Y (carbendazim, level 7, and diethofencarb, level 8) and T351I (zoxamide, level 8).
    • Bos1 and OS1, two entries of the same protein with 11 and 2 positions (12 distinct, as 373 is in both), were found at 99.9 % identity. They have the reference amino acid at every FungAMR position, so their verdict is wild type. The nuclear run did not extract cytochrome b, as expected, because the mitochondrion is not in contigs_final.fasta.
    • Cytochrome b. The mitochondrial run extracted a protein of 398 amino acids against the reference's 391, at 96.2 % identity. The protein has 7 residues the reference lacks, so ChroQueTas compared no position. The status is not evaluated, and the verdict -. The same run with the standard code (table 1) did worse, with 91.2 % identity, 17 gaps, and five tryptophans, encoded by TGA, read as stops. That is why the mitochondrial run gets its own code.

    Cytochrome b has insertions because of its introns. In B05.10 the gene has four introns, and two of them enclose a short exon of 12 codons, codons 132 to 143. One intron, of 1,366 bp, ends just before codon 132, and the other, of 1,205 bp, starts right after codon 143. Codon 142 is TGA, tryptophan in code 4, and codon 143 is GGT, glycine, the wild type. The next exon starts with codon 144.

    Neither intron starts with GT and ends with AG, as most introns do: the first reads TA...TG, the second TT...TG. Because miniprot penalises intron ends other than GT-AG (Li 2023), it ended both introns too early and translated their last nucleotides as protein. That gave 3 extra residues around codon 131, and 4 at codons 142 and 143, where the protein it built reads MMINAL instead of WG. Together they make the 7 residues the reference lacks.

    The intron matters beyond the alignment. Banno et al. (2009) found two forms of the B. cinerea cytochrome b gene among field isolates: one with three introns, and one with an additional intron between codons 143 and 144, the form B05.10 carries. All 88 isolates with that intron were sensitive to the QoI fungicide azoxystrobin. The authors suggested that G143A, the change known to make plant pathogens resistant to QoI fungicides, would keep the intron from splicing itself out. Grasso et al. (2006) found an intron at the same place in the rusts and in Alternaria solani, in which G143A has never been reported. For a strain like B05.10, a not evaluated cytochrome b is therefore the expected outcome, and G143A is not expected.

    Limits¶

    • Species. 57 species have a database folder. Any other species is not screened, and FunFlux does not fall back to a related species. The species says where to look instead.
    • Single mutations only. A combination of mutations that causes resistance only together, gene duplication or loss, aneuploidy and changes outside the protein are not detected (FungAMR and ChroQueTas).
    • Introns that miniprot cannot place. A gene with an intron whose ends are not GT...AG, as in the mitochondrial cytochrome b of B05.10, can come out with extra residues and stay not evaluated. The .faa then holds wrong residues around the misplaced junction (in B05.10, MMINAL where codons 142 and 143 should read WG), so do not read the position from it. Align the reference protein to the contig with the right genetic code instead, and read the position from the hit that covers it, since the hits follow the exons. BLAST+ is in the chroquetas environment: tblastn -query 00.resources/chroquetas/FungAMR_db/<species>/Cytochrome_b.faa -subject 02.assembly/{sample}/contigs_mitochondrial.fasta -db_gencode 4 -outfmt '6 qstart qend sstart send pident qseq sseq'. In B05.10 the hit for codons 132 to 143 reads YVLPYGQMSLWG, glycine at 143. The .paf (fields 6 to 9) shows which contig and region miniprot used.
    • Nonsense mutations. A premature stop codon at a FungAMR position comes out as no alignment, not as a FungAMR mutation, even where FungAMR lists the stop. FungAMR lists such a stop, written X, in 242 rows, most of them in Saccharomyces cerevisiae Fcy1 and Fur1, for 5-fluorocytosine. The .faa of the copy shows the stop as *.
    • An unconfirmed mitochondrion. When the contamination and mitochondrion screen splits the mitochondrion off but confirms no contig, cytochrome b is not evaluated, even if the mitochondrion sits unconfirmed among the contigs of contigs_final.fasta. mitochondria/mitochondrial_screen.tsv says why no contig was confirmed (Mitochondria).
    • The genetic code is one per run. mito_genetic_code sets one code for the mitochondrial run of every sample, so a batch of species with different mitochondrial codes needs auto.
    • The assembly. A target split across two contigs, or with a sequencing error at a FungAMR position, gives a second copy, a lower identity or a false call. Read the .paf and .blastp.txt files of a surprising call. A copy cut at a contig end can still pass the filter of step 2 while it lacks up to a quarter of the protein. The positions it lacks are no alignment, also where FungAMR lists a deletion, and a codon with N bases is position not found (FungAMR and ChroQueTas). Neither is a finding about the strain.
    • Paths without spaces. ChroQueTas uses its file paths without quotes, so a space in the path of the output folder or of the database breaks it. The job then stops with [FunFlux] amr_chroquetas: error: ... before ChroQueTas starts.
    • Evidence, not phenotype. A verdict sums up what FungAMR records about a change (Verdicts). It is not a susceptibility test, and does not replace one for a decision on a treatment or a spray programme. ChroQueTas itself ends each completed run with the reminder that its mutations must be weighed by their confidence scores.
